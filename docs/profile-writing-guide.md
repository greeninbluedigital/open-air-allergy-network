# Writing practice profiles (bios and FAQs)

Rules for the Short Bio, Extended Bio, FAQs and other copy OAAN writes for a
practice's page, e.g. trial profiles. Set by the owner 2026-10-08. Also apply
`OpenAirAllergyNetwork_STYLE_GUIDE.md` and the humanized-writing rules (lede
first, varied sentence length, no dashes or semicolons joining clauses, no
banned words). Third person, platform voice: never "we" for the practice.

## Facts
1. **Every fact about the practice must be stated clearly on the practice's
   own website.** No inferring, no filling gaps. Keep the source pages in the
   draft's notes.
   **Exception (2026-10-14): what the practice tells OAAN directly** by phone
   or email counts too, and wins when it contradicts the website, since
   practice sites often lag what the office actually does (e.g. Aspire's
   Arizona offices, Bay Area's Central Ave office). Log who said what and
   when in Verification Notes (column V), attribute it ("the practice says")
   and keep the other rules: no days or hours, no prices.

2. **No prices, payment or insurance, anywhere on the page** (bios and
   FAQs). That includes insurance accepted, self pay, direct pay or "direct
   care" labels, HSA/FSA, financing, payment plans and superbills. OAAN's job
   is the lead. How people pay is the provider's conversation when closing
   it, and payment details only give a consumer a reason to hesitate before
   submitting. Once a practice claims its page, it can say whatever it wants.
3. **Bios never say the practice treats children or adults.** Whether ILIT
   suits a child is between the provider and the family, and children aren't
   ILIT's audience. A "Does [Practice] treat children?" FAQ is optional: only
   when the practice's site strongly promotes treating kids and the profile
   needs more FAQs.
4. **No "only" claims** ("the only clinic in Iowa…"). They go stale the day a
   competitor starts offering ILIT, and nobody remembers to update them. "The
   first clinic in [region] to offer ILIT" is fine when the practice's site
   supports it (e.g. it says it's the only one today).
5. **Don't put hours in bios or FAQs.** Hours belong only in the Business
   Hours column, the one place to update when they change. The same goes for
   anything else that changes often.
6. No outbound links on paid or trial pages (the practice page is the
   endpoint).
7. **Never imply anyone can get ILIT.** No "available to all patients".
   Candidacy is a decision the provider and patient make together.
8. **Attribute, don't endorse (2026-10-09).** OAAN is an independent
   directory. The note under every bio says how OAAN confirmed the practice
   offers ILIT (directly, or from its own published information) and to check
   scheduling and costs with the practice (2026-10-25; the "not affiliated"
   wording was dropped because paying practices are planned). Write as a
   neutral reporter:
   - Credit practice facts and claims to the practice: "According to its
     website…", "The practice says…", "AllerPoint describes…", "Its website
     lists…". Vary the wording, and don't attribute every sentence: once a
     paragraph is framed as the practice's information, plain facts like the
     address or the number of injections can follow without a tag.
   - Always attribute claims of distinction: firsts, "one of the few",
     "pioneered", media coverage, a doctor's own words or opinions.
   - No OAAN praise or recommendation ("a great choice", "experienced team",
     "personal attention"). Describe, don't sell.
   - General ILIT facts OAAN stands behind (on the Learn pages) and local
     allergy facts from a named source (e.g. the AAFA report) need no
     practice attribution, since they aren't the practice's claims.
   - Keywords still belong: "fast allergy treatment" and "faster alternative
     to allergy shots" work as plain descriptions of ILIT's schedule
     ("three injections over about eight weeks, instead of years of allergy
     shots").

## Practice voice, for practices with a signed agreement (owner, 2026-10-28)
Once a practice signs OAAN's agreement (a $0 contract counts), its Short Bio
and Extended Bio can be written as the practice speaking to the reader
("we", "our office", "you"), with a marketing slant that helps the page
convert. First used on the Dayton demo (Providers row 8). What changes and
what doesn't:
- **Changes:** no "the practice says" attribution, a direct opening hook
  ("Don't want to spend years getting allergy shots?"), and a call to action
  ("Send us a message on this page or call…"). The practice's phone number
  can appear in the Extended Bio.
- **Still applies:** every fact comes from the practice's site or what it
  told OAAN; no prices, payment or insurance; no hours (Business Hours
  column only); no "only" claims; never imply anyone can get ILIT (the
  provider and patient decide together); describe ILIT accurately ("a course
  typically takes three injections about a month apart", never a promise of
  results); the style guide's punctuation and banned words.
- **FAQs and the OAAN note under the bio stay in OAAN's neutral voice.**
- Until the agreement is signed, a page in practice voice stays a demo
  (Demo/Example Listing = Y).

## Wording
- **Where the injection goes:** "a lymph node in the groin (just below the
  waist, near the hip)", never "groin" alone, which some readers take to
  mean the genitals (owner, 2026-10-20). Use the practice's own location if
  it differs (e.g. Columbia Allergy's A-ILIT uses the neck).

## FAQs
- **Focus on ILIT and allergy treatment**, the questions of someone deciding
  whether to contact this practice for ILIT. Skip side topics (food allergy
  programs, unrelated services) even if they'd draw traffic.
- **Include a local allergy FAQ**, e.g. "What allergies are common in central
  Iowa?" or "Can RapImmune treat ragweed allergy?", tying the area's real
  triggers (from a reliable source, noted in the draft) to what ILIT treats.
- A standard set to adapt: faster alternative to allergy shots; is [brand]
  FDA-approved (FDA-approved extracts, off-label route, if the site says so);
  who isn't a good fit; a local allergen question; how soon can I be seen; same-day test results (if stated);
  location (address and nearby areas, no hours); children only per rule 3.
- Each answer leads with something specific to this practice, never a
  restatement of the Learn pages.
- **Questions say "ILIT allergy treatment" (2026-10-30).** Whenever a
  question uses "ILIT", "allergy treatment" (or "intralymphatic
  immunotherapy") goes right next to it: "Is ILIT allergy treatment faster
  than allergy shots?", not "Is ILIT a faster alternative to allergy shots?".
  Answers can say plain "ILIT" once the question has set it up.
- **A practice's brand name for ILIT (QUICK-LI, ExACT Immunoplasty, AAT,
  Elaria Rapid Allergy Therapy) goes in one question at most,** because few
  people search it. Make that one "Are {Brand} and ILIT allergy treatment the
  same thing?", placed second, unless another answer already explains that
  the brand is the practice's name for ILIT. Every other question says "ILIT
  allergy treatment"; answers can still use the brand where the practice's
  wording calls for it.

## Keywords
Work in what people search, naturally: "allergist in [city]", "allergy doctor
[city]", "allergy testing [city]", "allergy shots", "faster alternative to
allergy shots", "fast allergy treatment", the practice's ILIT brand name, and
local allergens (ragweed, oak, grass, cedar). The Short Bio's first sentence
becomes the Google description: about 155 characters, standing on its own.

## Limits
Short Bio 600 characters, Extended Bio 2,000, FAQs about 8 to 10
(`docs/tiers-and-pricing.md`). Drafts live in `content-drafts/trial-profiles/`.
