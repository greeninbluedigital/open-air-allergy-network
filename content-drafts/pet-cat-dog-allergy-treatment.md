# Title
Pet Allergy Treatment: Cat & Dog Allergy Shots

# Meta Description
Sneezing around your cat or dog? Pet allergy treatment options include allergy shots and ILIT, a faster way to build tolerance to pet dander.

# Tags

- Pet allergies are triggered by proteins in an animal's dander, saliva, or urine — not just fur.

# Body

If being around a cat or dog reliably sets off sneezing, itchy eyes, or congestion, you're dealing with a pet allergy — one of the most common allergy triggers, and one people are often reluctant to address because it feels like the only fix is giving up the pet.

## What Causes Pet Allergies

Pet allergies come from proteins found in an animal's dander (flakes of skin), saliva, and urine — not the fur itself. Those proteins are small and light enough to stay airborne and settle into carpets, furniture, and bedding, which is why symptoms can show up even when the pet isn't in the room.

## Common Symptoms

Sneezing and nasal congestion, itchy or watery eyes, and skin reactions after direct contact are the most common signs. Symptoms tend to build the longer you're around the animal and can persist for a while after you've left the environment, since pet allergen lingers on soft surfaces.

## Managing Exposure

Some practical steps can reduce day-to-day symptoms: keeping pets out of the bedroom, using a HEPA air filter, washing hands after contact, and regular vacuuming with a HEPA-filter vacuum. These help manage exposure, but they don't change how your immune system responds to the allergen — that's what immunotherapy is for.

## Treatment Options

Antihistamines and nasal sprays can control symptoms in the moment. For a longer-term fix, allergy shots (SCIT) work by introducing small, increasing amounts of pet dander so your immune system gradually builds tolerance, typically over several years of ongoing treatment.

## A Faster Option: ILIT

Pet dander is one of the common allergens intralymphatic immunotherapy (ILIT) protocols are built around. Rather than years of under-skin injections, ILIT delivers allergen extract directly into a lymph node, guided by ultrasound, typically over a handful of visits across a few months. For someone whose main or only trigger is pet-related, that's worth asking an allergist about directly — it may mean a meaningfully shorter path to living more comfortably around the pets you already have.

# FAQ

Q: What causes pet allergies?
A: Proteins in an animal's dander, saliva, and urine — not the fur itself. These proteins are airborne and settle into fabric and furniture, which is why symptoms can persist even without the pet nearby.

Q: Do I have to give up my pet if I'm allergic?
A: Not necessarily. Many people manage pet allergies through a combination of exposure reduction (air filtration, keeping pets out of the bedroom) and treatment such as allergy shots or ILIT. Talk to an allergist about what's realistic for your situation.

Q: Can allergy shots help with cat or dog allergies specifically?
A: Yes — allergy shots can be formulated for pet dander specifically, building long-term tolerance the same way they do for other allergens like pollen or dust mites.

Q: How is ILIT different for treating pet allergies?
A: ILIT uses the same basic principle as allergy shots — introducing allergen to build tolerance — but delivers it into a lymph node rather than under the skin, typically over months instead of years.

Q: What are common pet allergy symptoms?
A: Sneezing, nasal congestion, itchy or watery eyes, and skin reactions after direct contact with the animal are the most common.
