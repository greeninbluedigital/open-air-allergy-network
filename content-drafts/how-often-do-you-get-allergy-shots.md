Standard allergy shots are given once or twice a week while your dose builds up, then every two to four weeks for three to five years. That's the schedule the [American Academy of Allergy, Asthma & Immunology](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shots-%28immunotherapy%29) (AAAAI) describes for subcutaneous immunotherapy (SCIT), which is what most people mean by allergy shots.

The shot itself takes seconds. The schedule is the real commitment, so it's worth looking at what it asks of your calendar before you start.

## The build-up phase: once or twice a week

Each shot in the build-up phase carries a slightly higher dose of your allergens than the last. The AAAAI says these shots are given about once or twice a week, and the phase takes four to six months. Some clinics run it longer. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/25194-allergy-shots) describes one to three shots a week for six to ten months.

The pace can also change along the way. If a shot causes a strong reaction, your allergist may repeat or lower the dose instead of moving up, which adds visits.

## The maintenance phase: every two to four weeks

Once you reach your target dose, visits spread out. The AAAAI says maintenance shots are typically given every two to four weeks and continued for three to five years.

This is where most of the time goes. At one shot a month, three to five years of maintenance works out to roughly 36 to 60 more visits.

## The part that isn't on the schedule

You don't leave right after a shot. The [AAAAI strongly recommends](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shot-tips) waiting in the office for 30 minutes, because serious reactions usually develop within that window.

Add the drive each way, and a quick shot becomes a standing hour or more on your calendar, every week at first. For many people, that time is the hardest part of allergy shots.

## How often can you get allergy shots?

Sometimes more than once a week. Allergists can speed up the build-up phase with accelerated schedules:

- **Cluster schedules** give several shots in one visit. The AAAAI says this can shorten build-up by at least one to two months.
- **Rush schedules** give many shots over a single day and can reach the maintenance dose in weeks. The risk of a reaction is higher, so rush dosing usually comes with medication beforehand and closer monitoring.

A [2023 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10527491/) in *The Journal of Allergy and Clinical Immunology: In Practice* compared the three approaches at one allergy practice. Patients on a standard weekly schedule took an average of about 31 weeks to reach their maintenance dose. Cluster patients took about 19 weeks, and rush patients about 17.

What you can't do is space doses however you like. Your allergist sets the plan, and a long gap between shots can mean stepping the dose back down and building up again.

## If weekly visits won't fit your life

Accelerated schedules shorten the start, not the whole course. Maintenance still runs for years.

Two other forms of immunotherapy change the math more.

**Allergy drops or tablets (SLIT)** go under the tongue at home, usually daily, with periodic check-ins at the office. The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/drug-guide/sublingual-immunotherapy-%28slit%29-allergy-tablets) says treatment may be needed for three years or longer. You trade most of the clinic trips for a daily routine.

**ILIT (intralymphatic immunotherapy)** injects the allergen directly into a lymph node, guided by ultrasound. The [trial that established it](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19001265/) used three injections four weeks apart, and a typical course today looks similar. ILIT is newer and hasn't been reviewed for FDA approval. Not every allergist offers it yet. If your calendar is what's keeping you from treatment, it's the option most worth asking about.

Our [comparison of allergy shots, allergy drops, and ILIT](/learn-about-ilit/allergy-shots-vs-allergy-drops-vs-ilit) lays out all three side by side.

## Before you commit to a schedule

Ask which build-up schedule the clinic uses, and whether cluster dosing is an option for you. If you travel often, ask how the clinic handles a missed shot. The answer tells you how much a busy month would really set you back.
