KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Oak made up about a fifth of all the pollen collected at 31 National Allergy Bureau stations in the U.S. and Canada, narrowly the top share in a 2019 study.
- Oak season starts earlier and lasts longer the farther south you live: February 11 on average in Houston, May 2 in Rochester, New York.
- Oak belongs to the birch group of pollens, so the raw fruits and nuts that bother people with birch allergy can bother some people with oak allergy.
- The stations with the most oak pollen in the 2019 study were in Texas and Georgia, led by Austin.
- There's no FDA approved allergy tablet for oak, so the immunotherapy options are allergy shots, allergy drops given off label, or ILIT.

FAQS
Q: When is oak pollen season?
A: It depends on how far south you live. A [2019 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6934246/) of National Allergy Bureau pollen counts found oak season started on February 11 on average in Houston and on May 2 in Rochester, New York. [Texas A&M AgriLife](https://agrilifetoday.tamu.edu/?p=307924) says oaks typically flower in March and April in Central Texas. Check your local pollen count at [pollen.aaaai.org](https://pollen.aaaai.org) to see when oak starts climbing where you live.

Q: What foods should you avoid with an oak allergy?
A: Only the foods that make your mouth itch or swell. Oak is in the birch group of cross-reacting tree pollens, according to a [2021 study](https://experts.mcmaster.ca/display/publication1959735), and the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/oral-allergy-syndrome-%28oas%29) links birch pollen with raw pitted fruits, carrots, peanuts, almonds and hazelnuts. Baking, microwaving or peeling the food can help, the AAAAI says. If nuts make your mouth itch, see an allergist, because that may signal a more serious nut allergy.

Q: Are there allergy shots for oak pollen?
A: Yes. Oak extract can be part of a course of allergy shots, which the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shots-%28immunotherapy%29) describes as four to six months of build-up shots on a traditional schedule, then maintenance shots typically every two to four weeks for three to five years. ILIT (intralymphatic immunotherapy) is a much shorter course of about three injections over about two months. To ask about it, use [Find a Provider](/find-an-ilit-provider) on OpenAirAllergyNetwork.com to locate an allergist who offers it.

Q: How long does oak pollen season last?
A: Anywhere from about three weeks to more than three months. In the [2019 pollen study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6934246/), the average oak season lasted 23 days in Seattle and 103 days in San Jose, California, and seasons ran longer at stations farther south. If your spring symptoms last well past your local oak season, another pollen such as grass may be involved, and an allergy test can sort that out.

Q: Can oak pollen make you sick?
A: It can make you feel run down, but it doesn't cause a fever. The [American College of Allergy, Asthma and Immunology](https://acaai.org/allergies/allergic-conditions/hay-fever/) (ACAAI) lists fatigue among hay fever symptoms, often from poor sleep with a blocked nose, and says hay fever doesn't cause a fever. A fever during oak season is a reason to call your doctor about an infection rather than blame the trees.

Q: Can oak pollen cause hives?
A: It's possible. The [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/allergies/allergic-conditions/skin-allergy/hives/) lists pollen among the triggers of hives, and its [hay fever page](https://acaai.org/allergies/allergic-conditions/hay-fever/) includes itchy skin among allergy symptoms. Hives have many other causes, so if they show up every oak season, mention the timing to an allergist.

Q: Can you have an oak allergy in the fall?
A: Not from oak pollen in most places. Oaks release pollen in late winter and spring, with average season starts from February to May in the [2019 pollen study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6934246/). Fall symptoms usually come from something else: the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/ragweed) says ragweed now pollinates from early August through October in many areas. Our [ragweed allergy guide](/blog/ragweed-allergy) covers fall season.

Q: What does oak pollen look like?
A: You'll usually notice the catkins, not the pollen. [Texas A&M AgriLife](https://agrilifetoday.tamu.edu/?p=307924) describes catkins as the worm-shaped male flower tassels that drop and pile up around oaks after they release pollen. The pollen itself is too small to see: the yellow dust on cars and decks is heavy pollen that falls near the tree, the [AAFA](https://aafa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/aafa-2026-allergy-capitals-report.pdf) says, and in pine country much of it is pine. A dusty windshield isn't a reliable oak count, so use a pollen count instead.

Q: Is oak pollen an allergen?
A: Yes, and a common one. The [Asthma and Allergy Foundation of America](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/pollen-allergy/tree-pollen-allergy/) (AAFA) calls oak very allergenic and says it grows in every state except Alaska and Hawaii. [UF/IFAS](https://gardeningsolutions.ifas.ufl.edu/?p=24975) names oak one of the most allergenic trees in Florida. If your symptoms peak with the oak bloom, ask for oak to be included when you're tested.

Oak pollen allergy is a common spring allergy in the U.S. because oak trees grow almost everywhere and most of them produce heavy loads of pollen. A [2019 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6934246/) of 31 National Allergy Bureau stations in the U.S. and Canada found that oak made up 19.6% of all the pollen collected, the top share in the study. Oak season starts as early as February in the South and as late as May in the North, and it brings the usual hay fever symptoms: sneezing, a runny or stuffy nose and itchy eyes. Oak rarely blooms alone: other trees such as elm release pollen in the same weeks, an allergist at [UT Health San Antonio](https://lsom.uthscsa.edu/pediatrics/2026/03/30/weather-works-how-the-south-texas-weather-plays-a-role-in-allergy-season/) points out, so spring symptoms can come from more than one tree.

Allergy medicine can ease those symptoms while the oaks are blooming. To treat the allergy itself, allergists offer allergy shots and a much shorter injection course called ILIT (intralymphatic immunotherapy). There's no FDA approved allergy tablet for oak or any other tree pollen.

## When to get care right away

Tree pollen can trigger asthma, with shortness of breath, coughing and wheezing, according to the [Asthma and Allergy Foundation of America](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/pollen-allergy/tree-pollen-allergy/) (AAFA). If you can't catch your breath or your rescue inhaler isn't helping, get emergency care.

Some people with tree pollen allergy also react to raw fruits, vegetables and nuts. The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/oral-allergy-syndrome-%28oas%29) says these reactions are usually mild but can rarely cause severe throat swelling or a whole body reaction called anaphylaxis. Throat swelling or trouble swallowing or breathing after eating means calling 911.

## Why oak pollen causes so many allergies

![A long sandy lane under an arching tunnel of live oak branches hung with Spanish moss, with fallen leaves along the edges](OAK_LIVE_OAK_PHOTO_URL "Live oaks hung with Spanish moss line a lane in the coastal South. In the 2019 pollen study, Atlanta's station counted the second most oak pollen of any station.")

Most oak trees produce heavy pollen loads, the 2019 study notes, and the stations with the most oak pollen were in Texas and Georgia. Austin's station alone collected 14.3% of all the oak pollen counted across the study's stations, followed by Atlanta at 8.6%, Waco at 8.4%, and Houston and San Antonio at 6.3% each. The table below shows the rest. The AAFA calls oak very allergenic and says it grows in every state except Alaska and Hawaii. In Florida, which has 11 oak species, [UF/IFAS](https://gardeningsolutions.ifas.ufl.edu/?p=24975) names oak one of the most allergenic trees.

The pollen that triggers oak allergy is too small to see. The yellow dust you see coating outdoor surfaces in spring is heavy pollen that falls near the tree, the [AAFA](https://aafa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/aafa-2026-allergy-capitals-report.pdf) explains, while the light pollen that rides the wind is what most people react to. Where pines are common, much of that dust is pine pollen, which forestry experts at [NC State](https://cnr.ncsu.edu/news/2024/04/allergies-back-dont-blame-all-trees/) say is too large and too low in protein to cause allergies. Oak, birch, maple, cedar and hickory all produce allergenic pollen, they add.

## When is oak pollen season?

Oak pollen season starts earlier the farther south you live. In the [2019 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6934246/), the average start of oak season ranged from February 11 in Houston to May 2 in Rochester, New York, almost three months apart.

![Map of the U.S. with colored dots at pollen stations showing when oak pollen season starts, from February in Texas to May in the Northeast and Pacific Northwest](OAK_START_MAP_URL "full:Average start of oak pollen season at National Allergy Bureau stations, 2003 to 2017. Southern seasons also last longer. Average oak seasons ran from 23 days in Seattle to 103 days in San Jose, California, and the authors found longer seasons at lower latitudes.

## Oak allergy symptoms

Oak allergy symptoms are the same as for other tree pollens. The [AAFA](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/pollen-allergy/tree-pollen-allergy/) lists:

- Runny or stuffy nose and postnasal drip
- Sneezing
- Itchy eyes, nose, ears or mouth
- Red, watery eyes and swelling around the eyes
- Shortness of breath, coughing or wheezing

The [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/allergies/allergic-conditions/hay-fever/) adds itchy skin and fatigue, which often comes from poor sleep with a blocked nose. Hay fever doesn't cause a fever, the ACAAI says. Symptoms that return when the oaks bloom and fade when they finish are the clearest sign that oak is involved, and our [allergies or a cold guide](/blog/allergies-or-a-cold) helps if you're unsure.

## Oak allergy foods to avoid

Oak belongs to the birch group of trees, whose pollen proteins are similar enough to cross react, according to a [2021 study](https://experts.mcmaster.ca/display/publication1959735) that also names alder and hazel in the group. Birch pollen is linked to oral allergy syndrome, also called pollen food syndrome, where raw fruits, vegetables or nuts make the mouth itch or swell. The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/oral-allergy-syndrome-%28oas%29) links birch pollen with pitted fruits, carrots, peanuts, almonds and hazelnuts. For oak specifically, the allergy test maker [Thermo Fisher](https://www.thermofisher.com/allergy/us/en/allergen-fact-sheets/oak-tree.html) lists foods such as apple, cherry, peach, hazelnut, peanut, carrot, celery, soy and strawberry.

You only need to avoid the foods that bother you. Baking or microwaving the food can help, because heat breaks down the proteins behind the reaction, and peeling it may help too, since the protein is often concentrated in the skin, the AAAAI says. Itching from nuts is the exception to treat seriously: see an allergist, because it may signal a more serious nut allergy. Our [oral allergy syndrome guide](/blog/oral-allergy-syndrome) has the full food chart.

## Testing for oak allergy

An allergist confirms a tree pollen allergy with your history, an exam and tests for specific tree pollens, the [AAFA](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/pollen-allergy/tree-pollen-allergy/) says. Oak and birch results tend to go together. In the 2021 study, people's levels of birch and oak IgE (the allergy antibody a blood test measures) were strongly correlated. Our guide to [allergy skin tests vs. blood tests](/blog/allergy-skin-test-vs-blood-test) explains both methods.

Test reports often use oak's scientific name. Oaks belong to the genus *Quercus*, [Thermo Fisher](https://www.thermofisher.com/allergy/us/en/allergen-fact-sheets/oak-tree.html) notes, and a [Quest Diagnostics allergy panel](https://www.questdiagnostics.com/content/dam/corporate/restricted/documents/hcp/about-our-tests/allergy-regional-profiles/Respiratory_Profile_Region_XIII.pdf) lists its oak test as "Oak (Quercus alba), t7", for white oak. If you see *Quercus* on a blood test report, that's your oak result, and our guide to [allergy blood test results](/blog/allergy-blood-test-results) explains the numbers.

## How to cut your exposure during oak season

1. **Check the pollen count.** The AAAAI's National Allergy Bureau posts counts at [pollen.aaaai.org](https://pollen.aaaai.org). Spend less time outside on high oak days.
2. **Keep windows closed.** That goes for the house and the car. The AAFA suggests air conditioning on high pollen days.
3. **Shower and change after time outdoors.** Pollen grains stick to most surfaces, NC State notes, so wash your hair and put your clothes in the laundry.
4. **Dry laundry inside.** The AAFA advises against an outdoor line during pollen season.

## Allergy medicine for oak season

Nasal steroid sprays are the single most effective type of medicine for hay fever, the [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/allergies/allergic-conditions/hay-fever/) says, and antihistamines work best taken before symptoms develop. The AAFA advises starting treatment at least two weeks before pollen season. In Houston, where oak season starts around February 11 on average, that means late January.

Medicine works only while you take it. It doesn't change the allergy, so next spring starts the same way.

## Longer term options for oak pollen allergy

**Allergy shots (SCIT)** can include oak extract. The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shots-%28immunotherapy%29) describes shots once or twice a week for four to six months on a traditional schedule, then maintenance shots typically every two to four weeks for three to five years. Our [guide to allergy shots](/learn-about-ilit/allergy-shots-and-allergy-immunotherapy) covers what that involves.

**Allergy drops and tablets (SLIT)** are limited for trees. The [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/allergies/management-treatment/allergy-immunotherapy/slit/) says the only FDA approved allergy tablets are for ragweed, grass and dust mites, and that allergy drops aren't FDA approved and are used off label in the U.S. The ACAAI also says research has found under the tongue treatment relatively safe and effective for tree pollen hay fever. The 2021 oak study tested a birch tablet that isn't FDA approved: in 634 people, it improved combined symptom and medicine scores during oak season by 25% compared with placebo. It was a look back at an earlier trial, but it suggests a birch group extract may help with oak too.

**ILIT allergy treatment** is a fast allergy treatment course and, for some people, a faster alternative to allergy shots. An allergist uses ultrasound to inject FDA approved allergen extract into a lymph node in the groin (just below the waist, near the hip), which is an off label use. A typical course is about three injections over about two months, the schedule from a [2008 trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2582048/). In that trial, the effect after three years was equivalent to three years of allergy shots, with fewer allergic side effects. It studied grass pollen, not trees, so if oak is your main allergy, ask a practice whether it treats oak with ILIT.

The [2026 allergen immunotherapy guideline](https://college.acaai.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/2026-Allergen-Immunotherapy-Practice-Parameter-Update.pdf) from the AAAAI and ACAAI describes a growing body of ILIT research, with randomized trials reported in 23 publications. That's still smaller than the evidence behind allergy shots, which the guideline traces back more than 100 years, and results vary from person to person. The [ILIT FDA FAQ](/learn-about-ilit#faq-fda) explains the off label question.
