KEY TAKEAWAYS
- A pollen count is the number of pollen grains in a cubic meter of air, reported separately for trees, grasses and weeds.
- "High" starts at a different number for each type: 90 grains for trees, 20 for grasses and 50 for weeds on the National Allergy Bureau scale.
- Read the line for the pollen you're allergic to. A high total count doesn't always mean much of your pollen is in the air.
- A station count usually describes the previous 24 hours. A forecast is an estimate for today.
- Allergy medicine works best when you start it before your season, so use past counts to plan your first dose. If medicine isn't enough, ILIT is a much shorter immunotherapy course than allergy shots, typically about three injections over about two months.

FAQS
Q: What is considered a high pollen count?
A: On the National Allergy Bureau scale used by local counting stations such as [Forsyth County, North Carolina](https://www.forsyth.cc/eap/air_quality/pollen.aspx), a count is high from 90 grains per cubic meter for trees, 20 for grasses and 50 for weeds. Very high starts at 1,500 for trees, 200 for grasses and 500 for weeds. [St. Louis County](https://pollenandmold.stlouisco.com/html/PollenLevels.html) says most people with any sensitivity to a pollen will likely have symptoms at the high level.

Q: How is pollen count measured?
A: A sampler, usually on a rooftop, collects particles from the air for 24 hours, and the sample then goes to a lab, the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/hay-fever-and-pollen-counts) explains. North Carolina's state sampler in Raleigh pulls in air for one minute out of every ten, and technicians count the grains under a microscope, [NC DEQ](https://ncwater.org/about/divisions/air-quality/air-quality-monitoring/pollen-monitoring) says. Grass is reported as one number because grass pollen grains look nearly the same under a standard microscope, according to the [University of Alaska Fairbanks Cooperative Extension](https://uaf.edu/ces/healthy-individuals/pollen-count/index.php).

Q: Does rain lower the pollen count?
A: For a while. [NC State University](https://cnr.ncsu.edu/news/2025/03/spring-allergies-what-you-need-to-know/) forestry professor Robert Bardon says rain washes pollen out of the air and temporarily lowers the count, and very humid mornings can weigh pollen grains down. Dry, warm, windy days tend to bring higher counts. Storms are a different case: [MedlinePlus](https://magazine.medlineplus.gov/article/what-triggers-seasonal-allergies) notes research suggesting thunderstorms can trigger or worsen allergy symptoms.

Q: What is the most accurate pollen count?
A: A count from a certified station. The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/hay-fever-and-pollen-counts) says its National Allergy Bureau stations all had to meet strict certification standards. Many apps and weather sites show forecasts instead, [MedlinePlus](https://magazine.medlineplus.gov/article/what-triggers-seasonal-allergies) notes. The AAAAI's [glossary](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/allergy,-asthma-immunology-glossary/pollen-counts-defined) says NAB counts are based on actual counts reflecting real-time weather, while forecasts are most often based on earlier years' pollen data and general weather forecasts.

Q: What is a pollen index?
A: Some agencies and apps turn the grain count into a score of their own. Forsyth County, North Carolina, for example, rates pollen on a [100 point scale](https://www.forsyth.cc/eap/air_quality/pollen.aspx), where 1 to 25 is low, 26 to 50 moderate, 51 to 75 high and 76 or more very high, and lists the grain count beside it. If a number looks odd, check whether you're reading a score or grains per cubic meter.

Q: What is a high mold count?
A: [St. Louis County](https://pollenandmold.stlouisco.com/html/PollenLevels.html) lists 13,000 to 49,999 mold spores per cubic meter as high and 50,000 or more as very high, with 6,499 or fewer counted as low. Mold numbers run far higher than pollen numbers, so they have their own scale. The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/outdoor-allergens-ttr) says outdoor mold spores peak in July in warmer states and October in colder ones, and can be found year-round in the South and on the West Coast.

Q: Why are my allergies bad when the pollen count is low?
A: The number you saw may not match your air. A station count usually covers the previous 24 hours ([NC DEQ](https://ncwater.org/about/divisions/air-quality/air-quality-monitoring/pollen-monitoring) collects from 9 a.m. to 9 a.m.), and the [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/allergies/allergic-conditions/pollen-allergies/) points out that you can be near a pollen that triggers you even on a low count day. Recent contact with other allergens can also affect symptoms, the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/outdoor-allergens-ttr) says. If you can't connect your symptoms to the counts, allergy testing can show what you're reacting to.

Q: Are there allergy shots for pollen allergy?
A: Yes. The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/outdoor-allergens-ttr) says an allergist may recommend allergy shots when pollen allergy symptoms continue or last many months of the year. Its [allergy shots page](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shots-%28immunotherapy%29) describes a build-up phase of about four to six months, then maintenance shots every two to four weeks for three to five years. ILIT allergy shots (intralymphatic immunotherapy) are a much shorter course, typically about three injections over about two months. To ask about it, use [Find a Provider](/find-an-ilit-provider) on OpenAirAllergyNetwork.com to locate an allergist who offers it.

For anyone trying to plan a day around their allergies, a pollen count is one of the most useful numbers in the weather report, because it tells you how many grains of pollen were floating in each cubic meter of outdoor air, broken out by trees, grasses and weeds. To read one, find the line for the pollen you're allergic to and compare it with that pollen's own scale. A count of 50 means something different for trees than for grass.

Once you know how bad today is likely to be, you can decide whether to keep the windows shut, when to start your allergy medicine and whether it's time for something longer lasting, like allergy shots, allergy drops or ILIT, a faster alternative to allergy shots.

## What a pollen count measures

The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/allergy,-asthma-immunology-glossary/pollen-counts-defined) defines a pollen count as the number of pollen grains in a cubic meter of air. The higher it is, the more likely people with hay fever are to have symptoms outdoors.

Counts come from samplers, usually on rooftops, that collect what's in the air for 24 hours. The sample then goes to a lab, where it's analyzed for pollen types and amounts, the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/hay-fever-and-pollen-counts) explains, and counts are reported separately for trees, grasses, weeds and mold. In the U.S., the AAAAI's [National Allergy Bureau](https://pollen.aaaai.org/) (NAB) is the only pollen and mold counting network certified by the AAAAI, and it posts station counts on its map.

At North Carolina's state station, each sample runs from 9 a.m. to 9 a.m. and describes the previous 24 hours, according to [NC DEQ](https://ncwater.org/about/divisions/air-quality/air-quality-monitoring/pollen-monitoring). The number you read this morning tells you about yesterday's air.

## Pollen count levels: what low, moderate, high and very high mean

Each pollen type has its own scale. Below are the National Allergy Bureau ranges, as published by counting programs in [St. Louis County](https://pollenandmold.stlouisco.com/html/PollenLevels.html) and [Forsyth County, North Carolina](https://www.forsyth.cc/eap/air_quality/pollen.aspx), in grains per cubic meter of air:

| Level | Trees | Grasses | Weeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 0 to 14 | 0 to 4 | 0 to 9 |
| Moderate | 15 to 89 | 5 to 19 | 10 to 49 |
| High | 90 to 1,499 | 20 to 199 | 50 to 499 |
| Very high | 1,500 or more | 200 or more | 500 or more |

Mold is counted in spores and runs on a much bigger scale: 13,000 to 49,999 spores per cubic meter is high in St. Louis County's table.

The levels aren't set by how much pollen it takes to make a person sneeze. St. Louis County explains that the NAB builds them from counts reported by all its stations, using percentiles, so "high" grass means high compared with typical grass days. That's why the grass line looks so low next to the tree line.

What each level means for people with allergies, per St. Louis County:

- **Low:** only people extremely sensitive to that pollen are likely to have symptoms.
- **Moderate:** many people sensitive to it will.
- **High:** most people with any sensitivity to it will.
- **Very high:** almost everyone with any sensitivity, and very sensitive people could have severe symptoms.

## How to read a pollen count in five steps

1. **Find a station count near you.** Start with the [NAB map](https://pollen.aaaai.org/). [MedlinePlus](https://magazine.medlineplus.gov/article/what-triggers-seasonal-allergies) also suggests local news, and some counties and states run their own samplers.
2. **Look at your pollen, not the total.** The [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/allergies/allergic-conditions/pollen-allergies/) points out that a high overall count doesn't always mean much of the pollen you're allergic to is in the air. If you don't know your triggers, allergy testing can tell you, and our guide to [reading allergy test results](/blog/allergy-test-results) explains the report.
3. **Check the number against that pollen's scale.** A reading of 50 grains is moderate for trees but high for grasses and weeds.
4. **Check the date and the weather.** The count usually covers yesterday. Dry, warm, windy days tend to push counts up, while rain washes pollen out of the air for a while, according to [NC State University](https://cnr.ncsu.edu/news/2025/03/spring-allergies-what-you-need-to-know/).
5. **Act on high days.** Stay inside when you can, and keep windows closed at home and in the car, with the air conditioning on, the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/outdoor-allergens-ttr) advises. After time outdoors, shower, wash your hair and change clothes. If you have to mow or spend time outdoors, MedlinePlus says an N95 mask gives the most protection. Our [allergy cleaning guide](/blog/how-to-clean-house-for-allergies) covers keeping pollen out of the house.

## Pollen count vs. pollen forecast

Many weather apps show a pollen forecast, not a count. The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/allergy,-asthma-immunology-glossary/pollen-counts-defined) says forecasts are mostly built from earlier years' pollen data and the general weather forecast, while NAB counts come from samples of the actual air. Use the forecast to plan today, and the station count to see what actually happened.

## Why allergies can flare on a low count day

The ACAAI notes you can still end up around a pollen that sets you off on a low count day. The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/outdoor-allergens-ttr) adds that symptoms can be affected by more than the day's number, including recent contact with other allergens and how sensitive you are. Pets and dust don't show up in a pollen or mold count at all.

## Use past counts to time your allergy medicine

Hay fever medicines work best when they're started before symptoms develop, the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/hay-fever-and-pollen-counts) says. The ACAAI's example: if your symptoms are usually worst in mid-April, start your medicine at the end of March. [MedlinePlus](https://magazine.medlineplus.gov/article/what-triggers-seasonal-allergies) says checking pollen counts can help you time that first dose. Our [tree pollen](/blog/tree-pollen-allergy), [cottonwood](/blog/cottonwood-fluff-allergies) and [ragweed](/blog/ragweed-allergy) guides cover the typical seasons.

## Longer term options for pollen allergy

If you check the count every day and your symptoms keep going, the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/outdoor-allergens-ttr) says an allergist may recommend allergy shots for pollen allergy symptoms that continue or last many months of the year.

**Allergy shots (SCIT)** are subcutaneous immunotherapy, given as injections under the skin. The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shots-%28immunotherapy%29) describes shots once or twice a week for four to six months, then maintenance shots every two to four weeks for three to five years.

**Allergy tablets and drops (SLIT)** go under the tongue. The [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/allergies/management-treatment/allergy-immunotherapy/slit/) says the FDA has approved tablets only for ragweed, northern pasture grasses such as timothy, and dust mites, and that allergy drops aren't FDA approved and are used off label in the U.S.

**ILIT allergy treatment (intralymphatic immunotherapy)** is a much shorter course. An allergist uses ultrasound to inject FDA approved allergen extract into a lymph node, usually in the groin (just below the waist, near the hip), typically about three injections over about two months. Giving extract that way is an off label use, and the research, while promising, is smaller than for allergy shots. The [Learn About ILIT](/learn-about-ilit) page covers how it works and its [safety](/learn-about-ilit#faq-safety), and our [comparison of allergy shots, allergy drops and ILIT](/learn-about-ilit/allergy-shots-vs-allergy-drops-vs-ilit) lays the three side by side.

To talk with an allergist about this fast allergy treatment option, [find an ILIT provider near you](/find-an-ilit-provider).
