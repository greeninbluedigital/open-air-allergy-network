KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Tampa's average outdoor humidity runs about 86% in the morning, so controlling indoor moisture comes first.
- Keep indoor humidity between 30% and 50%. Mold thrives above 50%, and dust mites like humidity above 50% too.
- In Florida, the air conditioner is also a dehumidifier, and a properly working one should keep humidity in check at a thermostat setting around 78°F.
- After a flood or leak, dry everything within 24 to 48 hours, and if you have a mold allergy, let someone else do the cleanup.
- Keep windows closed during pollen season and dry laundry indoors.

FAQS
Q: What humidity should my house be in Florida?
A: Between 30% and 50% if anyone in the house has allergies. The [AAFA](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/mold-allergy/) gives that range for mold allergy, and the [Florida Department of Health](https://www.floridahealth.gov/environmental-health/mold) says that while most experts suggest staying under 60%, below 50% is best for controlling both mold and dust mites. A hygrometer from the hardware store will tell you where your home stands.

Q: Does humidity make allergies worse?
A: It can, mostly by feeding indoor allergens. Dust mites like humidity above 50%, according to the [AAFA](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/insect-allergy/dust-mite-allergy/), and the AAFA's [mold allergy page](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/mold-allergy/) says some mold spores spread with fog or dew when humidity is high, which makes them easier to breathe in. If your symptoms get worse in a damp, humid or moldy room, that may point to a mold allergy.

Q: Does air conditioning help allergies?
A: Yes, in two ways. The [AAFA](https://aafa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/aafa-2026-allergy-capitals-report.pdf) recommends central air with a good filter and closed windows during pollen season, and [UF/IFAS Extension](https://blogs.ifas.ufl.edu/mrec/2026/07/31/water-wednesday-recap-understanding-florida-homes/) points out that a Florida air conditioner also pulls moisture out of the air. The AAFA's [dust mite page](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/insect-allergy/dust-mite-allergy/) names an air conditioner as one way to keep humidity in the 30% to 50% range.

Q: Can AC make allergies worse?
A: It can if mold grows in it. The [AAFA](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/mold-allergy/) warns that air conditioners and dehumidifiers can themselves be a source of mold. The [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) says to keep drip pans clean and drain lines flowing, and not to run an HVAC system you know or suspect has mold in it, since it could spread mold through the house.

Q: Should I run a dehumidifier in Florida?
A: Use one where the air conditioner can't keep up, such as a damp closet or a room the AC doesn't reach. If your central air needs a dehumidifier's help just to get humidity under 60%, the [Florida Department of Health](https://www.floridahealth.gov/environmental-health/mold) says to have the system examined for problems. The [AAFA](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/mold-allergy/) adds draining it often and cleaning the coils and bucket, so the dehumidifier doesn't grow mold of its own.

Q: How do you prevent mold in a Florida home?
A: Control moisture, since mold won't grow without it, says the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home). The [Florida Department of Health](https://www.floridahealth.gov/environmental-health/mold) says standing water can let common molds take hold within 24 hours, and it recommends venting shower and cooking moisture outside and checking around the air handler for standing water. Fix leaks right away, and keep indoor humidity no higher than 50%, as the [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/mold-health/about/index.html) advises.

Q: Is there a fast allergy treatment for mold and dust mite allergies?
A: Medicine works quickly but only while you keep taking it. Immunotherapy treats the allergy itself: the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shots-%28immunotherapy%29) describes allergy shots continuing for three to five years once you reach maintenance, while ILIT (intralymphatic immunotherapy) is typically about three injections over about two months, with a smaller body of research behind it. Ask an allergist whether it fits your allergens, and use [Find a Provider](/find-an-ilit-provider) on OpenAirAllergyNetwork.com to locate an allergist who offers it.

When you allergy-proof your home in the Tampa Bay area, humidity is the thing to beat, because the damp air that makes summer feel heavy also feeds the dust mites and mold behind year round indoor allergies. Tampa's outdoor humidity averages about 86% in the morning across the year, according to [NOAA normals](https://climatecenter.fsu.edu/products-services/data/other-normals/relative-humidity) published by Florida State's climate center, and the [AAFA](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/insect-allergy/dust-mite-allergy/) says dust mites like humidity above 50%. So most of allergy-proofing a Tampa home comes down to keeping that moisture outside, and the air conditioner does much of the work.

None of it takes a remodel. A humidity meter costs about $10 to $50 at many hardware stores, the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) says, and most of the rest is running the air conditioner well and acting fast after storms and leaks. If your nose still runs once the house is dry, an allergist can test for dust mites and mold, and immunotherapy treats the allergy itself, whether through allergy shots, allergy drops or ILIT, which for some people is a faster alternative to allergy shots.

## When to get care right away

People with asthma or a mold allergy may have severe reactions to mold, the [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/mold-health/about/index.html) says, and the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) notes mold can set off asthma attacks in people with asthma who are allergic to it. If you have trouble breathing during cleanup or afterward, or your rescue inhaler isn't helping, call 911 or get emergency care.

## Why humidity and allergies go together in Tampa Bay

Dust mites and mold, the two big indoor allergens, love damp air. The AAFA says dust mites thrive at around 70°F and above and like humidity over 50%, and that in a warm, humid house they can survive all year. Tampa's normal monthly average temperature stays above 70°F from April through October, according to the [National Weather Service](https://www.weather.gov/media/tbw/climate/normals/TPADailyTable.pdf).

Indoor and outdoor mold levels tend to be highest in the Southeast because of its warm, humid climate, the AAFA's [2026 Allergy Capitals report](https://aafa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/aafa-2026-allergy-capitals-report.pdf) says, and that report ranked Tampa 29th of the 100 most populous metro areas for pollen allergies. The AAFA's [mold allergy page](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/mold-allergy/) adds that air cleaners and filters can't reduce mold spores in a home that's too humid, so humidity comes first.

## Tampa humidity by month

## What you need to allergy-proof your home

- A hygrometer (humidity meter), which the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) says costs about $10 to $50 at many hardware stores
- A working central air conditioner, or a window unit and a portable dehumidifier for rooms it doesn't reach
- Zippered allergen covers for the mattress and pillows
- An N95 mask, goggles and gloves for any mold cleanup

## How to allergy-proof your home, step by step

1. **Measure your indoor humidity.** Put a hygrometer in the bedroom and anywhere that smells musty. Mechanical meters are often off by more than 20%, [UF/IFAS Extension in Sumter County](https://blogs.ifas.ufl.edu/sumterco/2022/10/04/check-indoor-humidity/) warns, so check yours: sealed in a plastic bag for at least 12 hours with a cup of salt and water, it should read 75%. Some smart thermostats show humidity too.
2. **Keep humidity between 30% and 50%.** That's the AAFA's range for both dust mites and mold, and the [Florida Department of Health](https://www.floridahealth.gov/environmental-health/mold) says below 50% is best for controlling both.
3. **Let the air conditioner dehumidify.** In Florida, the AC pulls moisture out of the air as well as cooling it, [UF/IFAS Extension](https://blogs.ifas.ufl.edu/mrec/2026/07/31/water-wednesday-recap-understanding-florida-homes/) explains, and a properly working system should still control humidity with the thermostat around 78°F. If your central air needs a dehumidifier's help just to get under 60%, the Florida Department of Health says to have the system checked.
4. **Keep the windows shut.** Florida nights can reach 80% to 100% humidity, UF/IFAS Sumter County notes, and open windows let all of it in. Closed windows also keep pollen out, as the [AAFA](https://aafa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/aafa-2026-allergy-capitals-report.pdf) advises during pollen season.
5. **Keep the AC itself clean.** The [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) says to keep drip pans clean and drain lines flowing, and the Florida Department of Health suggests checking around the air handler for standing water. Air conditioners and dehumidifiers can become a source of mold themselves, the [AAFA](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/mold-allergy/) warns. Our [cleaning guide](/blog/how-to-clean-house-for-allergies) covers which filter to use.
6. **Vent the bathroom and kitchen.** Run the bathroom fan during showers and for 15 to 20 minutes after, the AAFA says. The EPA recommends exhaust fans when cooking or running the dishwasher, and venting clothes dryers outside.
7. **Treat condensation as a warning.** Water collecting on windows, walls or pipes can be a sign of high humidity, the EPA says, so dry it right away and find the source.
8. **Start with the bed.** The AAFA recommends zippered allergen covers on the mattress and pillows and washing bedding weekly in hot water. Our [dust mite allergy guide](/blog/dust-mite-allergy) has the full plan. Where you can, the AAFA suggests solid-surface flooring and washable throw rugs over wall to wall carpet, which dust mites love.

## How to prevent mold in Florida homes after storms and leaks

Moisture control is the key to mold control, the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) says, and storms test it. Airborne mold spores have been reported to rise before, during and after storms, according to the [AAFA](https://aafa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/aafa-2026-allergy-capitals-report.pdf).

1. **Dry everything within 24 to 48 hours.** That's the window the [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/mold-health/about/index.html) and EPA give after a flood or leak. Carpet and upholstery that got soaked and can't be dried right away should come out, the CDC says.
2. **Check humidity when the power comes back.** Without electricity, nothing has been pulling moisture out of the air, and humidity is typically higher after a hurricane, [UF/IFAS Sumter County](https://blogs.ifas.ufl.edu/sumterco/2022/10/04/check-indoor-humidity/) says. Run the AC and a dehumidifier until the meter is back under 50%.
3. **Let someone without allergies clean.** The [Florida Department of Health](https://www.floridahealth.gov/environmental-health/mold) says people cleaning mold should be free of symptoms and allergies. Whoever does it should wear an N95 respirator, goggles and gloves, the [CDC's cleanup guide](https://www.cdc.gov/mold-health/communication-resources/guide-to-mold-cleanup.html) says.
4. **Scrub small spots, call in help for big ones.** The EPA's method for hard surfaces is detergent and water, then drying completely. You can usually handle a patch under about 10 square feet yourself, the EPA says. For anything bigger, or after a lot of water damage, it points to its remediation guidance, and any contractor you hire should have mold cleanup experience.
5. **Don't run a moldy AC.** If you know or suspect the HVAC system has mold in it, the EPA says not to run it, since it could spread mold through the house.

Renters should report leaks and moisture problems to the landlord or building manager right away, the EPA says.

## Longer term options for indoor allergies in Tampa Bay

A dry, cool house cuts exposure, but it won't make a dust mite or mold allergy go away. An allergist can test you to find which allergens are behind your symptoms, which tells you where to focus and whether immunotherapy fits.

Allergy medicine eases symptoms while you take it. Immunotherapy trains the immune system to tolerate specific allergens. Allergy shots have the longest track record: the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shots-%28immunotherapy%29) describes shots once or twice a week for four to six months, then every two to four weeks for three to five years. Allergy drops and tablets (sublingual immunotherapy, or SLIT) are taken under the tongue at home, and our [comparison of allergy shots, allergy drops and ILIT](/learn-about-ilit/allergy-shots-vs-allergy-drops-vs-ilit) covers which allergens each one treats.

ILIT (intralymphatic immunotherapy) is a much shorter course. An allergist uses ultrasound to inject FDA approved allergen extract into a lymph node at the top of the thigh, near the hip, an off label use of the extract. A course is typically about three injections over about two months, the schedule from the [2008 trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2582048/) that established it. Its research is promising but smaller than the research behind allergy shots. Our [Learn About ILIT](/learn-about-ilit) page explains how it works.
