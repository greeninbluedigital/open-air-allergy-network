A: ILIT retrains your immune system to ignore an allergen by giving small, controlled doses of it, the same idea behind allergy shots. The difference is that the dose goes straight into a lymph node, where the immune system learns what to respond to, so the training is faster and more efficient. Because it fixes the immune response instead of medicating symptoms, the benefit lasts long after the injections are done.

A: Usually three, each given about a month apart, so the whole course takes about two months. Allergy shots mean routine injections, weekly to monthly, for three to five years.

Q: How long do you wait after an ILIT allergy shot?

Q: Can I get ILIT injections if allergy shots gave me reactions?

Q: Are lymph node allergy shots a good option if you live far from an allergist?

If seasonal allergies like hay fever make you miserable every spring or summer, you may have heard of "allergy shots." They work well, but they take a big commitment: routine injections (weekly to monthly) over 3 to 5 years. A newer approach called intralymphatic immunotherapy (ILIT) aims to get a similar result with far fewer injections.

[ILIT vs. Traditional Allergy Shots: What a Few Months Buys You](/blog/ilit-vs-traditional-allergy-shots "card")

## What is ILIT allergy treatment?

Immunotherapy works by retraining your immune system. By exposing your immune system to small, controlled doses of your allergen, we teach the immune system to ignore your allergen, to stop overreacting. This leads to increased tolerance. Since we are fixing the immune system, rather than medicating symptoms, your benefit can last long after the injections are done!

The difference with ILIT is where the dose goes. Instead of going under the skin (allergy shots) or under the tongue (allergy tablets or drops), the allergen is injected directly into a lymph node, usually in the groin (just below the waist, near the hip). Lymph nodes are where your immune system "learns" what to respond to. Delivering the allergen directly into the lymph node trains the immune system more efficiently and rapidly.

## What does treatment involve?

- Usually just 3 injections, each given about a month apart.
- The provider uses an ultrasound to guide a very fine needle into a small lymph node just under the skin.
- Most people say it feels like a quick pinch, often less painful than a blood draw or traditional allergy shots.

## Does it work?

Research on ILIT has been growing over the last 20 years. The results so far are encouraging:

- Fewer symptoms, less medicine.
- Similar long-term response as a standard 3 to 5 year allergy injection program.

## Is it safe?

- The most common side effect is mild swelling, redness, or itching at the injection site.
- Serious allergic reactions have been rare in studies. Overall, ILIT has caused fewer side effects than standard allergy shots.
- As with any allergy immunotherapy, you will be watched for 30 to 60 minutes after each injection.

## What's the catch?

ILIT uses the same FDA-approved allergen extracts as allergy shots, but injecting them into a lymph node is still considered off-label in the United States. This means insurance typically will not cover the procedure. However, most patients have been able to use their HSA or FSA funds.

## Is ILIT allergy treatment right for me?

- The most common reasons for choosing ILIT are time and schedules. With ILIT you complete the course in just two months, vs. routine injections for 3 to 5 years.
- Distance from an allergy office: this can make routine injections more challenging, and ILIT provides an option with fewer visits.
- If you've been unable to complete a course of standard immunotherapy due to reactions, ILIT may be an option as this treatment has a significantly lower risk of reaction.
