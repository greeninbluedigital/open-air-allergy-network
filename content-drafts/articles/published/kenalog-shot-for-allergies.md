FAQS
Q: How long does a Kenalog injection last for allergies?
A: Usually several weeks. The [FDA label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cf970688-cd77-b95c-4bd9-c541590e9722) says the effect may be sustained over several weeks and that some hay fever patients stay symptom free through a whole pollen season after one injection. In a study of a similar depot steroid cited by the [EAACI](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6941282/), the effect on a stuffy nose lasted about four weeks.

Q: How often can you get a Kenalog shot for allergies?
A: There's no set schedule. The [FDA label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cf970688-cd77-b95c-4bd9-c541590e9722) gives no minimum time between shots and says dosing must be individualized, so the prescriber decides. U.S. allergy guidelines ([Rhinitis 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaci.2020.07.007)) recommend against depot steroid shots for hay fever, and a Danish study linked a shot every spring for three years or more to higher rates of diabetes and osteoporosis.

Q: Is a Kenalog shot a steroid?
A: Yes. Kenalog is the brand name for triamcinolone acetonide, a corticosteroid, which [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/druginfo/meds/a625028.html) describes as a drug that reduces inflammation. It isn't an anabolic steroid of the kind athletes misuse, and it isn't the same thing as an allergy shot, which is immunotherapy.

Q: Does a Kenalog shot cause weight gain?
A: It can. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/drugs/20715-triamcinolone-injection) lists increased appetite and weight gain among the common side effects of a triamcinolone injection, and [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/druginfo/meds/a625028.html) lists swelling or sudden weight gain. Fat gathering around the face or midsection is a different sign, a Cushing-like change, and Cleveland Clinic says to report it to your care team right away.

Q: Does Kenalog raise blood sugar?
A: It can. The [FDA label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cf970688-cd77-b95c-4bd9-c541590e9722) notes that corticosteroids may raise blood glucose, and people on diabetes medicine may need their doses adjusted. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/drugs/20715-triamcinolone-injection) lists increased thirst, frequent urination and blurry vision as signs of high blood sugar to report after the shot.

Q: Why don't doctors give steroid shots for allergies anymore?
A: Some still do, but allergy specialists' guidelines discourage it for hay fever. The joint [AAAAI and ACAAI guideline](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaci.2020.07.007) says depot steroid shots aren't recommended because of their potential risks, and a European panel ([EAACI](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6941282/)) strongly recommends against them except for very severe, treatment resistant symptoms. Steroid nasal sprays treat the same symptoms with far less steroid reaching the rest of the body.

Q: Where do you inject Kenalog?
A: For allergies and other whole body uses, the [FDA label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cf970688-cd77-b95c-4bd9-c541590e9722) calls for a deep injection into the buttock muscle. It says to avoid the upper arm (deltoid) because a dent from skin and fat thinning is much more common there, and to use a different spot for the next injection.

Q: What is an alternative to a Kenalog injection for allergies?
A: For daily symptoms, the [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/allergies/allergic-conditions/hay-fever/) calls steroid nasal sprays the single most effective drug class for hay fever. To change the allergy itself, immunotherapy is the option, and ILIT is the shortest course, typically three injections about a month apart. If you'd like to ask an allergist about ILIT, use [Find a Provider](/find-an-ilit-provider) on OpenAirAllergyNetwork.com to locate an allergist who offers it.

A Kenalog shot is an injection of triamcinolone acetonide, a long acting corticosteroid. For allergies, one shot can quiet hay fever for several weeks, and for some people a whole pollen season. There's no official answer to how often you can get one. The [FDA label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cf970688-cd77-b95c-4bd9-c541590e9722) leaves dosing to the prescriber, and the main U.S. allergy guideline recommends against steroid shots for hay fever because side effects build with repeated use. If you're looking for longer relief, allergy shots and other immunotherapy work on the allergy itself, and ILIT is the shortest course.

## When to get care right away

Like any drug, a Kenalog shot can cause an allergic reaction, and the label lists anaphylaxis among its rare adverse reactions. [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/druginfo/meds/a625028.html) says to get help right away for swelling of the lips, face, tongue or throat, or trouble breathing or swallowing. Call 911 for any of those.

## What a Kenalog shot is

Kenalog is a brand of triamcinolone acetonide, sold as Kenalog-40 and Kenalog-80. It's a steroid that calms inflammation throughout the body, which is why it eases a stuffy, itchy, runny nose. It's also what doctors call a depot steroid: a long acting injection that keeps working for weeks instead of hours, which the label reflects when it says one injection's effect may last several weeks. Another depot steroid you may see when searching is Depo-Medrol, a brand of methylprednisolone. The brand name simply drops the t. For allergies it's given as a deep injection into the buttock muscle, and the label warns against the upper arm because a dent from thinning fat and skin is much more likely there.

The label is narrower than many people expect. It lists seasonal and perennial allergic rhinitis only for "severe or incapacitating" allergies that haven't responded to adequate trials of usual treatment, and only when steroids by mouth aren't an option. So a Kenalog shot is FDA approved for hay fever, but only for those severe cases. A routine shot each season for milder symptoms falls outside the conditions the label lists. In everyday terms, it's meant as a backup, not a first step.

A Kenalog shot is also not an "allergy shot" in the usual sense. Allergy shots are immunotherapy, a series of small allergen doses that change how your immune system reacts. A steroid shot turns down the reaction for a while and then wears off.

## How long a Kenalog shot lasts for allergies

The label says the effect of an injection may last several weeks, and that some hay fever patients stay symptom free for an entire pollen season after one shot. A European Academy of Allergy and Clinical Immunology ([EAACI](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6941282/)) review describes a placebo controlled study of a different depot steroid, methylprednisolone (the drug in Depo-Medrol), in which relief from nasal congestion lasted about four weeks.

So the honest range is a few weeks to a season. It depends on the person and on how long the pollen they react to stays in the air.

## How often can you get a Kenalog shot for allergies?

No allergy society or FDA label sets a safe interval for repeat Kenalog shots for allergies. The label says dosing must be individualized, so the decision belongs to the doctor giving it.

What the research does show is that repeated shots add up. A [Danish registry study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rmed.2013.09.007) of 47,382 people with hay fever compared those who got at least one depot steroid shot every spring for three years or more with people treated with allergen immunotherapy. The steroid group had about a 50% higher rate of diabetes diagnoses and about a 20% higher rate of osteoporosis. That kind of study shows a link, not proof that the shots caused the problems. More recently, a 2026 [EAACI meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1111/all.70332) found that the risk of most steroid side effects rose significantly once a person's total steroid dose over time reached roughly 500 mg to 1 g of prednisolone, and urged doctors to keep lifetime totals down.

If you've been getting a shot every spring, it's worth asking your doctor to add up your total steroid exposure, including any prednisone courses for asthma or sinus infections.

## Kenalog shot side effects

Most of what people notice in the first days or weeks comes from the steroid spreading through the body. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/drugs/20715-triamcinolone-injection) lists increased appetite, trouble sleeping, weight gain, acne and headache as common side effects. MedlinePlus adds mood changes.

Some effects need a call to your doctor. Cleveland Clinic says to report signs of high blood sugar (more thirst, more urination, blurry vision), signs of infection such as fever or a wound that won't heal, and fat gathering around the face or waist. People with diabetes should plan for it, since the label notes steroids can raise blood glucose and diabetes medicine may need adjusting.

Two effects last longer than the relief does:

1. **Your stress hormone system.** Steroid shots can temporarily suppress the adrenal glands. In one small study the EAACI review cites, mild changes after a single injection were back to normal in four weeks. The label still says extra steroid may be needed during major stress, such as surgery, for up to a year after treatment, so tell any surgeon or dentist about the shot.
2. **Bones and skin.** The label lists osteoporosis among steroid risks, and a dent at the injection site from fat loss can happen, which is why the next shot should go in a different spot.

## Why many allergists reach for other treatments

The [AAAAI and ACAAI joint guideline on rhinitis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaci.2020.07.007) states that depot steroid injections are not recommended for hay fever because of their potential risks. The EAACI panel strongly recommends against them too, keeping them only as an option for very severe symptoms that nothing else controls. In the UK, the charity [Allergy UK](https://www.allergyuk.org/news/kenalog/) doesn't recommend the injections for hay fever, and in 2022 regulators ordered businesses to stop advertising them online.

The reason is that a safer drug does the same job. The [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/allergies/allergic-conditions/hay-fever/) calls steroid nasal sprays the single most effective drug class for hay fever, and they're designed to avoid the side effects of steroids taken by mouth or by injection. A shot sends a large dose through the whole body to treat one area, the nose.

## Kenalog shot vs. allergy shots

The two get confused because both are shots. They work in opposite directions.

A Kenalog shot suppresses inflammation for a few weeks, and the allergy is unchanged when it wears off. Allergy shots slowly teach the immune system to tolerate the allergen. The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shots-%28immunotherapy%29) says they may keep working even after treatment stops. The catch is time: build-up visits once or twice a week for four to six months, then a shot every two to four weeks for three to five years. Our guide to [how often you get allergy shots](/blog/how-often-do-you-get-allergy-shots) walks through that schedule.

## Longer term options

**Allergy medicine** is the everyday fix. Steroid nasal sprays come first for hay fever, and antihistamine pills, sprays and eye drops help with itching and sneezing.

**Allergy shots** change the allergy over three to five years of visits, and the AAAAI says they should be given in a health care setting.

**Allergy tablets** dissolve under the tongue at home. The ACAAI notes they treat only a few allergens for now: certain grass and ragweed pollens and house dust mites.

**ILIT (intralymphatic immunotherapy)** is the shortest immunotherapy course and, for some people, a fast allergy treatment option compared with years of shots. It's typically three ultrasound guided injections into a lymph node in the groin (just below the waist, near the hip), about a month apart. ILIT uses FDA approved allergen extracts given by an off label route, as our [Learn About ILIT](/learn-about-ilit) page explains. The research is promising but smaller than for allergy shots, and results vary.

Our [comparison of allergy shots, allergy drops and ILIT](/learn-about-ilit/allergy-shots-vs-allergy-drops-vs-ilit) lays out the differences side by side. If you've been relying on a steroid shot every season, an allergist can test for your triggers and talk through immunotherapy now. You can [find an ILIT provider near you](/find-an-ilit-provider) by zip code.
