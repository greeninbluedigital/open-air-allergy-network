KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Fish, snakes and turtles have no fur or feathers, which puts them at the top of the list for people with allergies.
- Cats rank as the worst pet for allergies, with dogs a close second.
- No dog or cat is truly hypoallergenic, whatever the breed, and that includes hairless cats.
- Rabbits, guinea pigs, hamsters, ferrets and horses can all cause allergy symptoms too.
- Fostering an animal before you adopt can show whether you react to it.

FAQS
Q: What pets don't cause allergies?
A: Pets without fur or feathers. The [AAFA](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/pet-dog-cat-allergies/) suggests fish, snakes and turtles as options for people with pet allergies, and says all dogs and cats can cause symptoms. Any animal with fur or feathers can trigger an allergy in someone sensitive to it.

Q: Are cat allergies more common than dog allergies?
A: Slightly, and the two are close. In U.S. national survey data from 2005 to 2006, [analyzed in a 2020 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7077112/), 12.1% of participants were sensitized to cat and 11.7% to dog. Being sensitized means a blood test found allergy antibodies (IgE) to that animal.

Q: Are guinea pigs, rabbits and hamsters hypoallergenic?
A: No. The [AAFA](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/pet-dog-cat-allergies/) names rabbits, guinea pigs and hamsters among the animals that can cause allergy symptoms. Several of their allergens belong to a protein family, lipocalins, that a [2024 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11172995/) says seem to account for most cross reactions among furry animals, so a cat or dog allergy may carry over, though that doesn't always happen.

Q: Can you be allergic to birds?
A: Yes. The [AAFA](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/pet-dog-cat-allergies/) says people can be allergic to animals with feathers, such as parrots and parakeets. Proteins in feathers and droppings can also cause bird fancier's lung, a lung inflammation in which, [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000109.htm) says, symptoms often start 4 to 8 hours after you leave the area where the trigger is.

Q: Can you be allergic to horses?
A: Yes. The [AAFA](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/pet-dog-cat-allergies/) lists horses among the animals that can cause allergy symptoms. A [2024 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11172995/) cites a study linking sensitization to both cat and horse with lipocalin proteins that cats, dogs and horses share, so people allergic to cats or dogs may test positive to horses as well.

Q: Can you be allergic to reptiles?
A: Reptiles have no fur or feathers, which is why the [AAFA](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/pet-dog-cat-allergies/) lists snakes and turtles as options for people with pet allergies. The bigger health issue with reptiles is germs: the [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-pets/about/reptiles-and-amphibians.html) says even healthy reptiles can carry Salmonella and that children younger than 5 shouldn't handle them.

Q: Is there a fast allergy treatment for pet allergies?
A: Allergy medicine gives the quickest relief. Allergy shots aim at the allergy itself but take years, with maintenance shots for three to five years after a build-up phase, per the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shots-%28immunotherapy%29). ILIT (intralymphatic immunotherapy) is a much shorter course, typically three injections over about two months. To ask whether it fits your allergy, use [Find a Provider](/find-an-ilit-provider) on OpenAirAllergyNetwork.com to locate an allergist who offers it.

## How we ranked

No health group ranks every pet by allergy risk, so this list follows what the sources do give. The groups come from the AAFA's pet allergy page, which names animals with fur first, then animals with feathers, and offers pets with neither as the option for allergic people. Cats rank above dogs on U.S. national survey data and the [American College of Allergy, Asthma and Immunology](https://acaai.org/allergies/allergic-conditions/pet-allergies/) (ACAAI) description of cat dander. No source orders rabbits, guinea pigs, hamsters, ferrets and horses against each other, so they share a rank.

## 1. Cats: the worst pet for allergies

Cats top the list. The ACAAI says cats produce multiple allergens, and quotes allergist Dana Wallace, MD, who calls cat dander the smallest dander of all, able to stay in the air for at least 30 minutes after it's disturbed. In U.S. survey data from 2005 to 2006, [analyzed in a 2020 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7077112/), 12.1% of participants were sensitized to cat, just ahead of the 11.7% sensitized to dog.

No cat breed gets you out of it. The ACAAI says all cats produce allergens and studies haven't shown that any cat is hypoallergenic. Our guide to [how long cat allergy symptoms last](/blog/how-long-do-cat-allergy-symptoms-last) explains why cat allergen lingers in a home for months.

### Are hairless cats good for allergies?

Hairless cats such as the Sphynx are a common hope for people with cat allergy, but going without fur doesn't remove the allergen. The ACAAI says cat allergens are found on the skin and in saliva as well as on the fur, and that the length of a cat's hair isn't associated with how much allergen it makes. A hairless cat still has skin and saliva, so a hairless cat allergy is the same allergy as any other cat allergy. If you have your heart set on one, spend time with that particular cat before you bring it home.

## 2. Dogs: almost as bad as cats for allergies

Dogs are a close second. The [American Academy of Allergy, Asthma and Immunology](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/dog-myths) (AAAAI) says about 10% of Americans are sensitized to dogs, and that no dog is truly allergen free.

One dog allergen is worth knowing about. The AAAAI says 20% to 30% of dog allergic people are sensitive to Can f 5, a protein that's largely reduced in neutered male dogs, so some people allergic only to Can f 5 may tolerate a female or a neutered male. Our article [Am I Allergic to My Dog?](/blog/allergic-to-my-dog) covers the signs and how to live with one.

## 3. Rabbits, guinea pigs, hamsters, ferrets and horses: not hypoallergenic

Small furry pets aren't a safe swap. The AAFA lists rabbits, guinea pigs, hamsters, ferrets and horses among the animals that can cause allergy symptoms.

If you already react to cats or dogs, be careful. Many furry animal allergens belong to one protein family, lipocalins, which a [2024 review in the Journal of Clinical Medicine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11172995/) says seem to account for most cross reactions among furry animals. It cites a study in which people sensitized to both cat and horse more often had antibodies to lipocalins shared by cats, dogs and horses, for example. The same review cautions that a positive cross reaction on a test doesn't always mean symptoms, which is why spending time with the actual animal matters.

## 4. Birds: feather allergy and bird fancier's lung

Birds rank better than furry pets but still carry allergens. The AAFA says people can be allergic to animals with feathers, such as parrots and parakeets.

Birds also cause a lung problem that isn't a typical allergy. [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000109.htm) describes bird fancier's lung, an inflammation caused by proteins in bird feathers or droppings and the most common type of hypersensitivity pneumonitis. Symptoms such as cough, fever, chills and shortness of breath often start 4 to 8 hours after you leave the area where the trigger is, which makes the bird easy to overlook. If you keep birds and have a cough or breathlessness that won't clear, tell your doctor about them.

## 5. Fish, snakes and turtles: the best pets for allergies

Pets without fur or feathers are the AAFA's suggestion for people with pet allergies, and fish, snakes and turtles are its examples.

Reptiles carry a different risk. The [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-pets/about/reptiles-and-amphibians.html) says even healthy reptiles can carry Salmonella, that children younger than 5 shouldn't handle them, and that homes with young children, older adults or people with weakened immune systems should consider another pet. Wash your hands with soap and water after handling one or cleaning its tank.

## Are hypoallergenic pets real?

Not among animals with fur. The AAAAI says breeds such as poodles, Bichon Frisés and Maltese are often promoted as hypoallergenic, yet even homes with Labradoodles had allergen levels similar to homes with other dogs. The ACAAI says the same of cats. The closest thing to a hypoallergenic pet is one with no fur or feathers at all. For dogs in detail, see [are hypoallergenic dogs real?](/blog/allergic-to-my-dog)

## Best pets for allergies and asthma

Pet allergy can set off asthma, so the same order applies with higher stakes. The AAFA says an asthma episode can begin within 15 to 30 minutes of breathing in an allergen, and the 2024 review notes that sensitization to several furry animals' lipocalins is associated with more severe allergy symptoms. Pets without fur or feathers are the lower risk choice. Our article on [allergies and chest tightness](/blog/can-allergies-cause-chest-tightness) explains when chest symptoms point to allergic asthma.

If you have trouble breathing around an animal and your rescue inhaler isn't helping, get care right away.

## How to choose a pet when you have allergies

1. **Get tested first.** The AAFA calls allergy testing the best way to find out if you're allergic to a specific type of animal. A [skin or blood test](/blog/allergy-skin-test-vs-blood-test) can show whether it's cats, dogs or something else.
2. **Foster before you adopt.** The AAFA suggests fostering a pet first to see whether you react.
3. **Meet the specific animal.** With dogs, sex and neutering can matter because of Can f 5, the AAAAI says, so time with the actual dog tells you more than its breed.
4. **Plan a pet free bedroom.** The AAFA recommends keeping pets and their cages and bedding out of bedrooms, and running an air cleaner with a HEPA filter at least four hours a day.

## Longer term options for pet allergies

Allergy medicine eases symptoms. Immunotherapy works on the allergy itself, building tolerance over time with gradually increasing doses of an allergen, as the ACAAI describes.

**Allergy shots (SCIT)** are the standard option, and the AAFA says they can be very effective if other treatments and steps aren't working. The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shots-%28immunotherapy%29) describes a build-up phase of shots once or twice a week that may take four to six months, then maintenance shots every two to four weeks for three to five years.

**ILIT (intralymphatic immunotherapy)** is a much shorter course and, for some people, a faster alternative to allergy shots. An allergist injects FDA approved allergen extract into a lymph node in the groin (just below the waist, near the hip), an off label route, typically three times over about two months. Research is promising but smaller than for allergy shots, and results vary. Our guide to [how long cat allergy symptoms last](/blog/how-long-do-cat-allergy-symptoms-last) covers what ILIT studies on cat allergen have found so far.

Our [comparison of allergy shots, allergy drops and ILIT](/learn-about-ilit/allergy-shots-vs-allergy-drops-vs-ilit) lays the options side by side. To talk with an allergist who offers the shorter course, [find an ILIT allergy treatment provider near you](/find-an-ilit-provider).
