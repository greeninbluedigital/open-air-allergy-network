KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Allergies can tighten your chest through allergic asthma, the most common type of asthma.
- Chest tightness isn't a hay fever symptom on the ACAAI's list. If your allergies reach your chest, ask a doctor about asthma.
- Chest pain you can't explain, or chest tightness with swelling of the face or tongue, is a reason to call 911.
- A rescue inhaler is the tool for a tight chest. Allergy pills and nasal sprays are part of allergic asthma care, alongside asthma medicine.
- National asthma guidelines recommend allergy shots as an add on treatment for allergic asthma in people 5 and older whose asthma is controlled.

FAQS
Q: Can allergies cause chest pain?
A: They can. The [AAFA](https://aafa.org/asthma/asthma-triggers-causes/allergic-asthma/) lists "chest tightness or pain" among allergic asthma symptoms. Chest pain also has causes that have nothing to do with allergies, and the [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/heart-disease/about/heart-attack.html) lists chest pain or discomfort as a major heart attack sign, so call 911 for chest pain you can't explain.

Q: Can allergies cause chest congestion?
A: Allergies mostly congest the nose, but asthma can make your chest feel full. During an asthma attack, the [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/asthma/about/index.html) says the airways swell, narrow and fill with mucus, so less air moves in and out. A heavy, congested chest with a cough or wheeze is worth a breathing test, which the [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/allergies/symptoms/cough/) says can help diagnose asthma.

Q: Can allergies cause wheezing?
A: Yes, through allergic asthma. The [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/asthma/types-of-asthma/allergic-asthma/) lists wheezing with chest tightness, coughing and shortness of breath. The [NHLBI](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/asthma/symptoms) describes wheezing as a whistling sound when you breathe out. New wheezing is a reason to get checked for asthma, since the NHLBI notes that other conditions can cause the same symptoms.

Q: Can post nasal drip cause chest tightness?
A: Postnasal drip can cause a cough that bothers you more at night, according to [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/symptoms/23082-postnasal-drip), but its symptom list doesn't include chest tightness. The [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/allergies/symptoms/cough/) says a cough that comes with wheezing, chest tightness or shortness of breath may be an asthma cough, and a breathing test can tell.

Q: Can allergies make it hard to breathe at night?
A: Yes. Asthma symptoms are often worse at night or in the morning, the [NHLBI](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/asthma/symptoms) says, and waking up at night from coughing is one of its signs that asthma is getting worse. Dust mites live in beds and pillows, per the [AAFA](https://aafa.org/asthma/asthma-triggers-causes/allergic-asthma/), so the bedroom is a good place to start cutting allergens.

Q: Do antihistamines help chest tightness?
A: Not as the main fix. Ohio State's [Wexner Medical Center](https://wexnermedical.osu.edu/asthma-care/asthma-medications) says rescue inhalers are the medicine used when your chest is tight or you're wheezing. The [AAFA](https://aafa.org/asthma/asthma-triggers-causes/allergic-asthma/) lists antihistamines among the allergy medicines used in allergic asthma care, separate from the asthma control and quick relief medicines a doctor may prescribe.

Q: What is the difference between allergic asthma and asthma?
A: Allergic asthma is asthma set off by allergens such as pollen, pets or dust, and [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/21461-allergic-asthma) calls it the most common type. Asthma can also flare from colds, exercise, cold air or laughing, per the [NHLBI](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/asthma/symptoms). The difference matters for treatment, because national asthma guidelines recommend allergy shots for allergic asthma specifically, the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/About/News/News/asthmaguidelines) reports.

Q: Can cat allergies cause chest tightness?
A: Yes. The [AAFA](https://aafa.org/asthma/asthma-triggers-causes/allergic-asthma/) lists cats, dogs, mice, rats and birds among allergic asthma triggers. Cat allergen also lingers in a home long after the cat is gone, which our article on [how long cat allergy symptoms last](/blog/how-long-do-cat-allergy-symptoms-last) explains.

Q: Is there a fast allergy treatment for allergic asthma?
A: For a tight chest right now, a rescue inhaler is the quick relief medicine, per [Ohio State](https://wexnermedical.osu.edu/asthma-care/asthma-medications). For the allergy behind it, immunotherapy is the longer term option. ILIT (intralymphatic immunotherapy) is the shortest course, three injections over about two months in the [2008 trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2582048/), though it has mostly been studied in hay fever. If you'd like to ask an allergist about it, use [Find a Provider](/find-an-ilit-provider) on OpenAirAllergyNetwork.com to locate an allergist who offers it.

Yes, allergies can cause chest tightness, through allergic asthma. Breathing in an allergen such as pollen, dust mites or pet dander makes the airways in your lungs tighten, and that can feel like something pressing on your chest, [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/21461-allergic-asthma) explains. Allergic asthma is the most common type of asthma, and Cleveland Clinic says about 60% of Americans with asthma have allergies. Plain hay fever is different. The American College of Allergy, Asthma and Immunology's [hay fever symptom list](https://acaai.org/allergies/allergic-conditions/hay-fever/) covers the nose, eyes, mouth, throat, tiredness and a cough, not a tight chest. So if your allergies seem to reach your chest, ask a doctor whether asthma is part of the picture.

## When to get care right away

Chest pain or discomfort you can't explain needs a 911 call, not a guess about allergies. The [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/heart-disease/about/heart-attack.html) lists it as a major heart attack sign, sometimes with shortness of breath. Chest tightness that comes with trouble breathing, wheezing, hives, or swelling of the face, eyes or tongue can be anaphylaxis, a severe allergic reaction. [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000844.htm) says to call 911 and use epinephrine right away if you have it.

If you have asthma, the [National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/asthma/attacks) (NHLBI) says to call 911 when your medicines aren't relieving an attack or breathing is still very hard after treatment. In a young child, drowsiness, confusion or a blue tint to the lips is an emergency.

## Why allergies can cause chest tightness

In allergic asthma, your immune system treats a harmless allergen as a threat. The [Asthma and Allergy Foundation of America](https://aafa.org/asthma/asthma-triggers-causes/allergic-asthma/) (AAFA) explains that it releases IgE antibodies, which can inflame the airways. During an asthma attack, the [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/asthma/about/index.html) says the airway walls swell, the airways narrow and mucus clogs them. Less air gets through, and that squeeze can feel like tightness in your chest.

The AAFA's list of common triggers:

- Dust mites, which live in beds, carpets, furniture and fabrics
- Mold, which grows where there's moisture, like a damp basement or a wet shower
- Animals, including cats, dogs, mice, rats and birds
- Pollen from trees, grasses and weeds
- Cockroaches

Allergens aren't the only fuel. The [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/asthma/types-of-asthma/allergic-asthma/) says colds and other viral infections, exercise, airborne irritants, stress and the weather can all make allergic asthma worse. Our guides to [dust mite allergy](/blog/dust-mite-allergy) and [tree pollen allergy](/blog/tree-pollen-allergy) cover two of the biggest triggers in detail.

## Allergic asthma symptoms

Chest tightness is one symptom among several. The AAFA lists these allergic asthma symptoms:

- Shortness of breath
- Coughing, which the ACAAI notes comes especially at night, during exercise or when you laugh
- Chest tightness or pain
- Wheezing, a whistling sound when you breathe
- Waking up at night with symptoms
- A drop in your peak flow reading, if you use a peak flow meter
- Trouble doing physical activities

You may also have the familiar allergy signs, such as sneezing, a stuffy or runny nose and red, itchy eyes, according to the AAFA. The [NHLBI](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/asthma/symptoms) adds that asthma symptoms come and go, sometimes within the same day, and are often worse at night or early in the morning.

## Chest tightness and shortness of breath: is it asthma?

Only testing can say. The NHLBI points out that other conditions can cause the same symptoms, and the ACAAI's [cough page](https://acaai.org/allergies/symptoms/cough/) names reflux (GERD), chronic bronchitis and smoking among the common causes of a lingering cough. A cough that comes with wheezing, chest tightness or shortness of breath may be an asthma cough, the ACAAI says.

To confirm asthma, Cleveland Clinic says doctors use breathing tests such as spirometry. A [skin test or blood test](/blog/allergy-skin-test-vs-blood-test) then shows which allergens are involved. An allergist can do both, and knowing your triggers helps you weigh the long term options below.

## How to relieve chest tightness from allergies

1. **Use your rescue inhaler if you have one.** Rescue inhalers are the medicine for a tight chest or wheezing, per Ohio State's [Wexner Medical Center](https://wexnermedical.osu.edu/asthma-care/asthma-medications). The NHLBI says to use it as soon as symptoms start and follow your asthma action plan. No inhaler and a severe attack? Cleveland Clinic says to call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department.
2. **Get away from the trigger.** Leave the room with the cat, or come inside on a high pollen day. Limiting exposure to your allergens is part of allergic asthma care, according to the AAFA.
3. **Treat your nose too.** The AAFA lists steroid nasal sprays, antihistamines and saline rinses as part of allergic asthma treatment. They work on the allergy side, and the AAFA lists them separately from asthma medicine.
4. **Ask for a written asthma action plan.** The AAFA describes it as a plan made with your doctor that says when and how to treat symptoms. Needing your rescue inhaler more often, or waking at night, means your asthma may be getting worse, the NHLBI says. Tell your doctor.
5. **Cut allergens where you sleep.** Cleveland Clinic suggests allergen proof bedding and regular cleaning. Our [dust mite guide](/blog/dust-mite-allergy) has a step by step plan for the bedroom.

## Longer term options for allergic asthma

**Asthma and allergy medicine** is the foundation. The AAFA describes a daily control medicine plus a quick relief inhaler, and Cleveland Clinic notes that injected biologics are an option for moderate or severe allergic asthma. Medicine controls symptoms while you use it.

**Allergy shots** treat the allergy itself. The 2020 national asthma guidelines from an NHLBI expert panel recommend them as an add on to standard asthma medicine for people 5 and older with allergic asthma, as long as the asthma is controlled when shots start and throughout treatment, the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/About/News/News/asthmaguidelines) reports. Shots start once or twice a week for four to six months, then continue every two to four weeks for three to five years, per the [AAAAI's allergy shot guide](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shots-%28immunotherapy%29).

**Allergy tablets** dissolve under the tongue and are approved for certain grass and ragweed pollens and house dust mites, according to the ACAAI. The same 2020 guidelines don't recommend under the tongue immunotherapy as an asthma treatment, so tablets are mainly a hay fever option.

**ILIT (intralymphatic immunotherapy)** is the shortest immunotherapy course, and for some people a faster alternative to allergy shots. An allergist injects FDA approved allergen extract into a lymph node in the groin (just below the waist, near the hip), an off label use that our [Learn About ILIT](/learn-about-ilit) page explains. The [2008 trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2582048/) behind it gave adults with grass pollen hay fever three injections over two months, compared with 54 shots over three years. Its research is promising but smaller than for allergy shots, it has mostly been studied in hay fever, and the AAAAI's summary of the 2020 asthma guidelines doesn't mention it. Whether it may help your breathing is a question for an allergist, and our Learn page [answers it briefly](/learn-about-ilit#faq-asthma).

Our [comparison of allergy shots, allergy drops and ILIT](/learn-about-ilit/allergy-shots-vs-allergy-drops-vs-ilit) sets the options side by side. If your chest tightens every allergy season, talk with an allergist now about getting your asthma under control and treating the allergy behind it.
