KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Allergy shots are allergy immunotherapy: rising doses of your own allergens teach your immune system to stop overreacting.
- They treat pollen, dust mite, mold, pet dander and stinging insect allergies, but not food allergies.
- Expect weekly visits for several months, then roughly monthly shots for three to five years, with a 30 minute wait after each one.
- About 80% of people see significant improvement, and lasting relief is most likely after at least three years of shots.
- ILIT is typically three injections into a lymph node over about two months, a much shorter course backed by lower quality research so far.

FAQS
Q: Are allergy shots IM (intramuscular) or subcutaneous?
A: Subcutaneous. That's where the medical name, subcutaneous immunotherapy (SCIT), comes from: the shot goes into the layer just under the skin, not into muscle. The [FDA label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cbe1d24d-df77-4940-baf8-623993fa731b) for one standardized dust mite extract says injections are given subcutaneously, preferably in the arm, and warns that it should not be injected into a vein.

Q: Can you get allergy shots for food allergies?
A: No. [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876) says allergy shots aren't available for food allergies or for long-lasting hives, and [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/25194-allergy-shots) adds medication and latex allergies to the list shots don't treat.

Q: Can you get allergy shots while pregnant?
A: You can usually keep going if you started before the pregnancy, but starting during pregnancy isn't recommended, according to the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/asthma/asthma-and-pregnancy). If you're already on shots, the AAAAI's advice is simply to let your doctor know you're pregnant.

Q: Who should not get allergy shots?
A: [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/25194-allergy-shots) says shots are for people over 5, and that anyone taking beta blockers should ask their allergist whether shots are safe for them, since those drugs can keep epinephrine from working in some people. The [FDA extract label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cbe1d24d-df77-4940-baf8-623993fa731b) adds that people with unstable or steroid dependent asthma, or with heart disease, face greater risk if a reaction happens.

Q: Can you get allergy shots while sick?
A: Usually not on that day. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/25194-allergy-shots) says to hold off with a fever of 100°F or higher, a cold or the flu, or an asthma attack, and its [allergist interview](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/allergy-shot-reactions/) advises waiting until you're well.

Q: What happens if you stop allergy shots early?
A: You may give up some of the lasting benefit. The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shots-(immunotherapy)) says lasting relief is most likely when shots are completed for at least three years. If you skip scheduled doses, [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876) says you may have to go back to lower doses to prevent a serious reaction.

Q: Do allergy shots help with asthma?
A: They can when allergies trigger the asthma. [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876) lists seasonal allergic asthma among the conditions shots are used for, and the [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/allergies/management-treatment/allergy-immunotherapy/) says immunotherapy reduces the inflammation behind both rhinitis and asthma. Asthma also raises the risk of a reaction to a shot, which is why Mayo Clinic asks you to tell the staff before a shot if you aren't feeling well, especially if you have asthma.

Q: How is intralymphatic immunotherapy (ILIT) different from allergy shots?
A: The allergen goes into a lymph node instead of under the skin, and the course is far shorter. In a [2008 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19001265/), people with grass pollen allergy got either three injections into a lymph node over two months or 54 allergy shots over three years, and three years later the two groups' tolerance was about the same. If you'd like to ask about it, use [Find a Provider](/find-an-ilit-provider) on OpenAirAllergyNetwork.com to locate an allergist who offers it.

Allergy shots are the most widely used form of allergy immunotherapy, a treatment that gives you small, gradually rising doses of the things you're allergic to until your immune system learns to stop overreacting to them. Unlike antihistamines or nasal sprays, which ease symptoms, shots aim at the allergy itself, and [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876) describes the usual course as a series lasting three to five years.

That long course is why it helps to see the whole picture before you start. This guide covers how allergy shots work, which allergies they treat, what the build-up and maintenance phases look like, how well they work and how safe they are, and how they compare with allergy drops, allergy tablets and ILIT (intralymphatic immunotherapy), a faster alternative to allergy shots. Each section links to a longer article where we've covered a piece in more depth.

## When to get care right away

Hives away from the injection site, throat tightness, wheezing, chest tightness or fainting after an allergy shot can signal a serious reaction, according to the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shots-(immunotherapy)) and [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/allergy-shot-reactions/). If it starts after you leave the office, use your epinephrine autoinjector if you were prescribed one and return to the clinic or go to an emergency room, as [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876) advises.

## How do allergy shots work?

Each shot carries just enough allergen to alert your immune system without causing symptoms, and the dose rises over time so your body gets used to it, [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876) explains. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/25194-allergy-shots) puts it as shifting your body's response to an allergen from inflammation to tolerance. The medical name is subcutaneous immunotherapy, or SCIT, because the shot goes just under the skin.

Allergy medicine works differently: pills and sprays help with a stuffy or runny nose, sneezing and itching, while immunotherapy changes how your immune system reacts. The [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/allergies/management-treatment/allergy-immunotherapy/) calls it the only treatment available that does, which is also why it may help prevent new allergies and asthma from developing. The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shots-(immunotherapy)) says allergy shots may provide lasting relief even after treatment stops.

## Who allergy shots are for, and testing first

[Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876) says allergy shots may be a good choice when:

- Medicines don't control your symptoms well, or cause side effects that bother you
- You can't avoid what triggers your allergies
- Your allergy medicines interact with other medicines you need
- You want to cut down on long-term allergy medicine
- You're allergic to insect stings

The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shots-(immunotherapy)) adds a few practical questions to weigh: how long your allergy season runs, how severe your symptoms get, and whether you can commit the time a multi-year course takes. Shots are an option once a board-certified allergist determines that allergens in your environment, such as grass pollen or dust mites, are behind your symptoms.

Before your first shot, a skin or blood test confirms that your symptoms come from an allergy and shows which specific allergens cause them, according to [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876), and those results decide what goes into your shots. Our article on [allergy skin tests vs. blood tests](/blog/allergy-skin-test-vs-blood-test) explains the two, and [Allergy Test Results Explained](/blog/allergy-test-results) walks through reading a skin test.

## What allergies do allergy shots help with?

Allergy shots treat allergies you breathe in and allergies to stinging insects. [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876) groups them this way:

| Type | Common triggers |
|---|---|
| Seasonal allergies | Pollen from trees, grasses and weeds, including seasonal allergic asthma |
| Year-round indoor allergies | Dust mites, cockroaches, mold and pet dander |
| Insect stings | Bees, wasps, hornets and yellow jackets |
| Not treated by shots | Food allergies and long-lasting hives (urticaria) |

*Source: [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876). [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/25194-allergy-shots) adds medication and latex allergies to the list shots don't treat.*

If you react to several of these, your shots can cover them together: [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/allergy-shot-reactions/) says your provider tailors the cocktail of allergens in your shots to the allergies you have. ## The two phases: build-up and maintenance

**Build-up** comes first. You get shots one or more times a week, and each dose is a little stronger than the last. How long it lasts depends on the clinic: the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shots-(immunotherapy)) says four to six months of shots once or twice a week, [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876) three to six months, and [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/25194-allergy-shots) six to 10 months. So plan on three to 10 months.

**Maintenance** starts once you reach your target dose. Visits spread out to every two to four weeks, per the AAAAI, and continue for three to five years. Cleveland Clinic says people with severe allergies may stay on maintenance longer than five years.

Some clinics shorten build-up by giving several shots in one visit. That gets you to maintenance and relief sooner, but [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876) says it also raises the risk of a serious reaction. Our [allergy shot schedule guide](/blog/how-often-do-you-get-allergy-shots) counts the visits on standard, cluster and rush schedules, and covers what happens when you miss one.

## What an allergy shot visit is like

Allergy shots are usually given in the upper arm, according to [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876), though [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/25194-allergy-shots) says the thigh or hip is sometimes used. Mayo Clinic says to tell the staff before the shot if you feel unwell, especially if you have asthma, or if you had symptoms after your last shot.

Afterward you stay in the clinic for at least 30 minutes so the staff can watch for a reaction. Mayo Clinic also says to skip vigorous exercise for at least a few hours after a shot. The AAAAI says shots should be given in a health care setting with the staff and equipment to treat reactions, and it doesn't recommend giving them at home.

## Do allergy shots work?

For most people, yes: about 80% of people see significant improvement in their allergy symptoms, and about 60% have permanent benefits after three to five years of shots, according to [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/25194-allergy-shots). The [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/allergies/management-treatment/allergy-immunotherapy/) calls allergy shots the most commonly used and most effective form of allergy immunotherapy.

Cleveland Clinic says most people notice fewer symptoms after six to eight months, and [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876) says the most noticeable improvement often comes in the second year. By the third year, most people no longer have bad reactions to their allergens. The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shots-(immunotherapy)) says lasting relief is most likely after at least three years of shots, and that if you haven't improved after a year of maintenance, your allergist should look at other options with you.

After a few years of successful treatment, some people have no allergy problems even after the shots stop, while others need ongoing shots, Mayo Clinic says. Our article on [whether there's a one-time allergy shot](/blog/one-time-allergy-shot) covers how long results last.

## Are allergy shots safe? Side effects and risks

The most common side effect is swelling, redness or irritation where the shot went in. [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/allergy-shot-reactions/) says the swelling can range from pea sized to golf ball sized and is usually gone within 48 hours. Whole body symptoms such as sneezing, a stuffy nose or hives are less common, [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876) says.

Severe reactions (anaphylaxis) are rare, and deaths are exceedingly rare, according to the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shots-(immunotherapy)). Allergist Mark Aronica told Cleveland Clinic that serious reactions are generally considered to be under 1%, and that the risk drops significantly once you reach maintenance. Anaphylaxis often starts within 30 minutes of a shot, which is the reason for the wait, though [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876) notes it sometimes starts later.

People with asthma are more likely to have a reaction, [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/allergy-shot-reactions/) says, so a shot should wait if you're having an asthma attack. Beta blockers, which treat heart and circulation conditions, can keep epinephrine from working in some people, and [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/25194-allergy-shots) says anyone taking them should ask their allergist whether shots are safe. Cleveland Clinic also says allergy shots have no long-term risks or complications.

For what's in an allergy shot and how to lower your risk of a reaction, see [What's in Allergy Shots?](/blog/whats-in-allergy-shots)

## Allergy shots vs. steroid shots (Kenalog)

A steroid shot such as Kenalog is a different drug from an allergy shot. Kenalog is triamcinolone acetonide, a corticosteroid that calms inflammation, and it contains no allergen. Its [FDA label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cf970688-cd77-b95c-4bd9-c541590e9722) says the effect may last several weeks, and it limits use for allergies to severe or incapacitating cases that haven't improved with adequate trials of conventional treatment. The joint [AAAAI and ACAAI rhinitis guideline](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaci.2020.07.007) goes further and recommends against these depot steroid injections for hay fever because of their potential risks.

Allergy shots work the other way around. They hold small amounts of your own allergens, and an [allergy extract label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cbe1d24d-df77-4940-baf8-623993fa731b) lists no steroid among the ingredients. Instead of calming symptoms for a few weeks, they change how your immune system reacts over years of treatment. Our [Kenalog guide](/blog/kenalog-shot-for-allergies) covers steroid shot side effects, and [Is There a One-Time Allergy Shot?](/blog/one-time-allergy-shot) compares the two in more detail. 

## Allergy shots for kids

Children as young as 5 can get allergy shots, according to the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shots-(immunotherapy)), and [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/25194-allergy-shots) says anyone over 5 can get them. The AAAAI says shots may also lower a child's risk of developing asthma.

## Allergy shots vs. drops, tablets and ILIT allergy treatment

**Allergy medicine** such as antihistamines and nasal sprays eases symptoms without changing the allergy underneath. [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876) suggests looking at allergy shots when those medicines don't control your symptoms well or cause side effects that bother you.

**Allergy tablets** go under the tongue. They're a form of sublingual immunotherapy (SLIT), and the [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/allergies/management-treatment/allergy-immunotherapy/) says tablets are the only sublingual therapy the FDA has approved. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/25194-allergy-shots) lists them: Odactra for dust mites, Oralair for mixed grasses, Grastek for Timothy grass and Ragwitek for short ragweed. They only cover those allergens. Liquid **allergy drops** aren't FDA approved, Cleveland Clinic notes.

**ILIT allergy treatment** puts FDA approved allergen extracts into a lymph node in the groin (just below the waist, near the hip), guided by ultrasound. Using a lymph node is an off label route, which our [ILIT FAQ](/learn-about-ilit#faq-fda) explains. A course is typically three injections over about two months, which makes it a fast allergy treatment course next to years of shots, though not instant relief. In a [2008 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19001265/) of 165 people with grass pollen allergy, three ILIT injections over two months gave about the same tolerance after three years as 54 allergy shots over three years, with fewer side effects. The paper's corresponding author is named as an inventor on an ILIT patent.

A [2025 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12714667/) of seven earlier reviews was more cautious, finding the clearest benefit for grass pollen and mixed allergies, less for dust mite allergy alone, and limited effect beyond a year. It said low quality evidence holds back its findings. ILIT allergy shots caused more reactions at the injection site but significantly fewer whole body reactions than standard allergy shots. Our [ILIT vs. traditional allergy shots](/blog/ilit-vs-traditional-allergy-shots) article goes deeper.

The [comparison of allergy shots, allergy drops and ILIT](/learn-about-ilit/allergy-shots-vs-allergy-drops-vs-ilit) puts all of them side by side, and you can [find an ILIT provider near you](/find-an-ilit-provider) by zip code.
