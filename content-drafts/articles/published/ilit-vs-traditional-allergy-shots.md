FAQS
Q: What are the side effects of intralymphatic immunotherapy?
A: Mostly mild reactions where the needle goes in. A [2025 review of seven systematic reviews](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12714667/) found local reactions were more common with ILIT than with placebo, while whole body reactions were no more common than with placebo, and no deaths were reported. In the [2008 head to head trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2582048/), 6 of 58 ILIT patients had mild reactions and none had a severe one.

Q: Are lymph node allergy shots the same as ILIT?
A: Yes. "Lymph node allergy shots" and "lymph node allergy injections" describe intralymphatic immunotherapy, where the allergen extract goes into a lymph node, usually at the top of the thigh near the hip, guided by ultrasound. Regular allergy shots go under the skin of the upper arm instead, as the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shots-%28immunotherapy%29) describes.

Q: Is ILIT as effective as allergy shots?
A: In the one large trial that compared them directly, symptom improvement was similar after one year and after three years ([Senti, 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2582048/)). That trial studied grass pollen allergy only, and the [2025 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12714667/) of the wider research rated most of it low quality. Allergy shots have decades more evidence behind them.

Q: Do ILIT results last as long as allergy shots?
A: Nobody knows yet. The 2008 trial found no difference between the two groups three years after starting, but the [2025 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12714667/) notes that long term data on ILIT are limited. Ask any practice you're considering how its own patients have done over time.

Q: Why does ILIT need fewer injections than allergy shots?
A: The allergen goes straight to the immune system's processing center. The [2008 trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2582048/) authors point out that only a small fraction of an under the skin injection ever reaches the lymph nodes, while an injection into a node delivers the whole dose there. That's why the trial's ILIT patients needed three small doses instead of 54 larger ones.

Q: What's the best alternative to allergy shots?
A: It depends on your allergies and how much time you can give treatment. Allergy drops or tablets are taken at home every day for years, and ILIT is the shortest course, about three injections over two months. ILIT (intralymphatic immunotherapy) and traditional allergy shots work the same basic way: small doses of what you're allergic to, given over time, so your immune system stops overreacting. The difference is where the dose goes and how long treatment takes. Allergy shots go under the skin for three to five years. ILIT goes into a lymph node, and a typical course is about three injections over two months.

That shorter course is what draws people to ILIT. What you give up is a much smaller body of research and fewer practices that offer it.

## ILIT vs. allergy shots at a glance

| | ILIT | Allergy shots (SCIT) |
|---|---|---|
| Where the dose goes | A lymph node at the top of the thigh, near the hip | Under the skin of the upper arm |
| Typical schedule | Typically three injections, a month apart | Weekly or biweekly for months, then every two to four weeks for three to five years |
| In use since | 2008 (first major trial) | [1911](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9707371/) |
| Reactions | More mild injection site reactions, fewer whole body reactions | Fewer local reactions, more whole body reactions |

## How many visits each one takes

The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-shots-%28immunotherapy%29) describes allergy shots in two phases. During build-up, you get shots once or twice a week for four to six months as the dose rises. Then come maintenance shots every two to four weeks for three to five years, and you wait in the office after each one in case of a reaction. Our article on [how often you get allergy shots](/blog/how-often-do-you-get-allergy-shots) counts the visits.

ILIT is far shorter. In the [2008 trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2582048/) that compared the two directly, ILIT patients got three injections, at the start, week 4 and week 8. The allergy shot group got 54 injections over three years.

## Why ILIT needs so few injections

ILIT puts the allergen where the immune system processes it. The 2008 trial's authors explain that only a small fraction of an injection under the skin ever reaches the lymph nodes, while an injection into a node delivers the whole dose there. The doses were tiny by comparison too. Across the whole course, ILIT patients received less than a thousandth of the total allergen the allergy shot group did.

An allergist finds the lymph node with ultrasound and uses FDA approved allergen extract. Injecting it into a lymph node is an off label use, which our [ILIT safety FAQ](/learn-about-ilit#faq-safety) explains.

## How the results compared

In the 2008 trial, 165 people with grass pollen allergy were randomly assigned to ILIT or allergy shots. Four months in, the ILIT group already tolerated about ten times more grass pollen in a nasal test, while the allergy shot group hadn't changed yet. By one year, the shot group had caught up, and there was no difference between the groups at one year or at three years. Symptoms improved in both groups by about the same amount. In the first pollen season, fewer ILIT patients needed antihistamine tablets (23 of 54, against 35 of 53).

The wider research is less clear cut. A [2025 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12714667/) pulled together seven systematic reviews of ILIT for hay fever. The benefit was clearest for grass pollen and for mixed allergies and weaker for dust mite allergy alone, and long term data were limited. The authors rated most of the evidence low quality, mainly because the trials were small.

So the head to head result is promising, and it comes from one trial in one allergy. The paper also discloses that its lead author is named as an inventor on an ILIT patent. Allergy shots have decades of studies across many allergies behind them.

## Side effects and comfort

Reactions differed in an important way. In the 2008 trial, 2 people in the allergy shot group had severe asthma reactions that needed a hospital stay, and 18 had mild allergic reactions. In the ILIT group, 6 had mild reactions such as hives or swelling, and no one had a severe reaction. The 2025 review found the same pattern across studies: ILIT causes more local reactions (mild discomfort or swelling) where the needle goes in, but significantly fewer whole body reactions than allergy shots.

ILIT also hurt less than many people expect. Patients in the trial rated the ILIT injection at about 10 on a 100 point pain scale, compared with about 17 for having blood drawn. The authors say the pain comes from the needle passing through the skin, since lymph nodes have few nerves that sense pain.

## Finishing treatment

Every ILIT patient who started the 2008 trial got all three injections. In the allergy shot group, 32 of the 54 who started were still going after three years. Most of the rest were people the researchers lost touch with, which says something about how hard three years of appointments can be.

## Which one fits you

Allergy shots make sense if you want the treatment with the longest track record, live near an allergist who offers them, and can keep regular appointments for several years. ILIT may suit you if years of visits aren't realistic, if you've stopped allergy shots before, or if you'd travel a few times rather than commute weekly.
