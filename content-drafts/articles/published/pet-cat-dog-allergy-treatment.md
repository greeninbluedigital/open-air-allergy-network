KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Pet allergy comes from proteins in dander, saliva and urine, not fur, and no cat or dog breed is truly hypoallergenic.
- Living with a pet may bring steady congestion instead of sudden symptoms, which makes it easy to miss that the pet is the cause.
- Allergy shots for cat and dog allergy usually start with one or two shots a week for 4 to 6 months, then a shot every four weeks for 3 to 5 years.
- None of the FDA approved allergy tablets treats cat or dog allergy, so pet immunotherapy means shots or ILIT.
- ILIT is typically three lymph node injections over about two months, but its research on cat and dog allergy is small and mixed.

FAQS
Q: Do dog allergy shots work for humans?
A: They can, though the evidence is thinner for dogs than for cats. In a [2020 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31132032/) of 66 people treated with high dose shots for cat or dog allergy, symptoms improved overall, and people treated with cat extract did significantly better than those treated with dog extract. The [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/allergies/allergic-conditions/pet-allergies/) still lists allergy shots among the treatments for dog allergy.

Q: How long do cat allergy shots take to work?
A: Most people start to notice fewer symptoms six to eight months in, and the full benefit can take 12 months, according to [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/25194-allergy-shots). That timing is for allergy shots in general. Relief comes well before the course ends: for pet allergy, maintenance shots run three to five years, per [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/pet-allergy/diagnosis-treatment/drc-20352198).

Q: Is there a pill for cat allergies?
A: For symptoms, yes. [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/pet-allergy/diagnosis-treatment/drc-20352198) lists antihistamine tablets sold without a prescription, such as fexofenadine (Allegra Allergy), loratadine (Claritin) and cetirizine (Zyrtec Allergy), plus montelukast (Singulair) by prescription. There's no allergy tablet that retrains your immune system to cats: the four FDA approved tablets [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/25194-allergy-shots) lists treat dust mites, grasses and ragweed.

Q: Can you get allergy shots for cats and dogs at the same time?
A: Yes. Allergy shots aren't one size fits all: [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/allergy-shot-reactions/) says your provider tailors the cocktail of allergens in your shots to the allergies you have, so cat and dog can go into the same treatment along with other allergens your tests turn up.

Q: Do I have to get rid of my cat if I'm allergic?
A: Not always. The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/conditions-treatments/allergies/pet-allergy) says giving up a pet isn't always necessary and that an allergist can build a plan that may let you keep it. If nothing else works and the pet does go, the AAAAI says it can take six months or more for the dander to clear from the home.

Q: Can pet allergies cause asthma?
A: Pet allergy can trigger asthma attacks in people who also have asthma, which the [AAFA](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/pet-dog-cat-allergies/) calls allergic asthma. [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/pet-allergy/symptoms-causes/syc-20352192) says people with asthma and pet allergy can find their asthma hard to manage and may be at risk of attacks that need urgent or emergency care.

Q: Can pet allergies go away with exposure?
A: Living with a pet can make the allergy look like it's gone when it isn't. The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/conditions-treatments/allergies/pet-allergy) says long term exposure may bring ongoing congestion instead of the sudden symptoms of a short visit, which can make it seem as if the pet isn't causing problems. The [AAFA](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/pet-dog-cat-allergies/) adds that allergies can change over time, and an allergy test is the way to see where you stand now.

Q: Can intralymphatic immunotherapy treat cat or dog allergy?
A: It's been studied, with mixed results. A [2012 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22464647/) using an engineered cat protein, not a standard extract, raised nasal tolerance 74 fold after three injections, while a [2021 placebo controlled trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8178859/) of tyrosine adsorbed extracts for dust mite, cat and dog found symptom scores no better than placebo. If you'd like to ask an allergist about it, use [Find a Provider](/find-an-ilit-provider) on OpenAirAllergyNetwork.com to locate an allergist who offers it.

For most people, pet allergy treatment comes in layers: cutting down the dander in your home, allergy medicine for day to day symptoms, and, when those aren't enough, allergy shots that train your immune system to tolerate cat or dog allergen over three to five years. [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/pet-allergy/diagnosis-treatment/drc-20352198) says those shots are usually used when simpler treatments aren't satisfactory, and the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/conditions-treatments/allergies/pet-allergy) says allergy shots have a proven track record as an effective long-term treatment for a pet dander allergy.

Whichever of those layers you need, it helps to know what's behind the sneezing first. For the allergy itself, the options are allergy shots and ILIT (intralymphatic immunotherapy), a faster alternative to allergy shots that's still early in its research for pets.

## When to get care right away

If you have asthma, a pet allergy can trigger an asthma episode that begins within 15 to 30 minutes of breathing in the allergen, the [Asthma and Allergy Foundation of America](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/pet-dog-cat-allergies/) (AAFA) says. [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/pet-allergy/symptoms-causes/syc-20352192) says to seek emergency care if wheezing or shortness of breath quickly gets worse, and to seek care if you're short of breath with very little activity.

## What causes pet allergy (it isn't the fur)

![A smiling woman on a couch with a black and white dog in front and an orange and white cat behind her](https://res.cloudinary.com/qruprn0t/image/upload/v1795460903/pet-cat-dog-allergy-treatment-woman-on-couch-with-pets.jpg)

A pet allergy is a reaction to proteins, not hair. The AAFA says the proteins come from a pet's dander (dead skin cells), saliva and urine, and that fur collects the dander and can also carry dust mites, mold and pollen. Dander is a particular problem because it's so small, [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/pet-allergy/symptoms-causes/syc-20352192) explains: it stays in the air with the slightest draft, collects in upholstered furniture and sticks to clothes.

That's why pet allergens turn up where no pet has ever lived. The AAFA says people carry them on their clothing into classrooms and workplaces, and the [American College of Allergy, Asthma and Immunology](https://acaai.org/allergies/allergic-conditions/pet-allergies/) (ACAAI) adds that a pet allergy can cause constant symptoms through exposure at work, school or day care even when no pet is present.

Cats and dogs are behind the most common pet allergies, but Mayo Clinic says any animal with fur can, including rabbits, hamsters and guinea pigs. Pets without fur, such as fish and reptiles, rarely cause it.

The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/conditions-treatments/allergies/pet-allergy) says there are no truly hypoallergenic breeds of dogs or cats, and that dander isn't affected by the length of the fur or how much an animal sheds. Our ranking of the [best and worst pets for allergies](/blog/pets-ranked-by-allergies) goes animal by animal.

## Pet allergy symptoms

Most pet allergy symptoms look like hay fever. [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/pet-allergy/symptoms-causes/syc-20352192) lists sneezing, a runny or stuffy nose, itchy, red or watery eyes, postnasal drip, cough, facial pressure and pain, trouble sleeping and an itchy nose, roof of the mouth or throat. In children, it adds frequent upward rubbing of the nose. A scratch or lick can leave the skin red and itchy or raise hives, the AAFA says.

When a pet allergy feeds into asthma, Mayo Clinic says you may also have trouble breathing, chest tightness and wheezing. Our article on [allergies and chest tightness](/blog/can-allergies-cause-chest-tightness) explains when that points to allergic asthma.

Living with the animal can blur the picture. The AAAAI says long term exposure may bring steady nasal congestion instead of the sudden symptoms you'd get on a short visit, which can make it look as if the pet isn't the problem. For cats in particular, symptoms can also come back hours after you leave, which our article on [how long cat allergy symptoms last](/blog/how-long-do-cat-allergy-symptoms-last) explains.

### Pet allergy vs. dust, pollen and mold allergy

The symptoms themselves rarely tell you which allergen is to blame. The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/indoor-allergens-ttr) lists sneezing, stuffiness, a runny nose and itching for indoor allergens like pet dander and dust mites, and its [outdoor allergens page](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/outdoor-allergens-ttr) lists the same ones for pollen and mold. The better clues are when and where your symptoms show up:

| Allergy | When symptoms tend to show up | Where | Clue that points to it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pet (cat or dog) | Within minutes of being around the animal, or steady congestion if you live with one | Around the animal and its bedding, and on pet owners' clothing | Red, itchy skin or hives where a pet scratched or licked you |
| [Dust mites](/blog/dust-mite-allergy) | Year round | Bedding, upholstered furniture and carpet | Symptoms that don't follow the seasons |
| [Pollen](/blog/how-to-read-a-pollen-count) | Trees in early spring, grass in late spring and early summer, weeds in late summer and fall | Outdoors | Symptoms that rise and fall with the season |
| [Mold](/blog/mold-allergy) | Outdoors, spores peak in July in warmer states and October in colder ones; indoors, any time | Damp, humid rooms like basements, and outdoors | Symptoms that get worse in a damp room |

*Sources: AAAAI indoor and outdoor allergen pages and pet allergy page, AAFA pet and mold allergy pages. In warmer places, the AAAAI says pollen can be in the air year round.*

Any of these allergens can also trigger asthma flare ups in people with allergic asthma, the AAAAI says. An allergy test, covered next, sorts out which ones are behind your symptoms.

## Pet allergy test: confirming the cause

An allergy test tells you whether the pet is the cause, and the AAFA calls testing the best way to find out if you're allergic to a specific type of animal. The ACAAI says it's a good idea to be tested even if you're sure, because something else may be behind the symptoms, such as dust or pollen carried in a pet's coat.

In a skin test, tiny amounts of allergen extract, including animal proteins, are pricked into the skin, usually on the forearm, and checked after 15 minutes, according to [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/pet-allergy/diagnosis-treatment/drc-20352198). When a skin condition or a medicine rules out a skin test, a blood test can look for the same allergy antibodies. Our guide to [allergy skin tests vs. blood tests](/blog/allergy-skin-test-vs-blood-test) compares the two.

For dogs, testing can get more specific. The [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/dog-myths) says it can show whether you react to Can f 5, a protein from unneutered male dogs, and some people allergic only to that protein may tolerate a female or a neutered male.

## Living with a pet: how to cut down the allergen

![A white dog lying on a gray couch with a plaid blanket draped over its head and a quilted cover over the cushions](https://res.cloudinary.com/qruprn0t/image/upload/v1795461217/pet-cat-dog-allergy-treatment-dog-on-couch-blanket.jpg)

Mayo Clinic suggests talking with your doctor about whether reducing exposure, rather than finding your pet a new home, may be enough. These steps come from the AAFA, the AAAAI and Mayo Clinic:

1. **Keep the pet out of the bedroom.** You spend about eight hours a day there, the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/conditions-treatments/allergies/pet-allergy) notes. Keep the pet's bedding out too, and the door closed.
2. **Run a HEPA air cleaner.** The [AAFA](https://aafa.org/allergies/types-of-allergies/pet-dog-cat-allergies/) suggests at least four hours a day, especially where people sleep. The ACAAI notes that shutting a pet in one room won't keep its allergen in that room, and it also says to keep the pet out of the bedroom.
3. **Keep pets off fabric furniture.** If that won't happen, the AAFA suggests slipcovers or blankets you wash weekly in hot water.
4. **Hand off the brushing and the litter box.** Ask someone without a pet allergy to brush the pet outdoors and clean the litter box or cage, as the AAFA and Mayo Clinic suggest.
5. **Bathe the pet weekly.** [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/pet-allergy/diagnosis-treatment/drc-20352198) and the AAFA both suggest a weekly bath, which the AAFA says may reduce airborne allergen. For dogs, the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/dog-myths) says the effect wears off within two to three days, which [our dog allergy article](/blog/allergic-to-my-dog) covers.
6. **Wash your hands after petting.** Mayo Clinic says to wash right after touching a pet.

Our room by room guide to [cleaning your house for allergies](/blog/how-to-clean-house-for-allergies) covers vacuuming, floors and filters. If the pet does have to go, the AAAAI says it can take six months or more for the dander to clear.

## Pet allergy medicine for humans

[Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/pet-allergy/diagnosis-treatment/drc-20352198) lists these options for pet allergy symptoms:

| Medicine | Examples Mayo Clinic names | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Antihistamine tablets | Fexofenadine (Allegra Allergy), loratadine (Claritin), cetirizine (Zyrtec Allergy), levocetirizine (Xyzal Allergy), sold without a prescription | Relieves itching, sneezing and runny nose |
| Steroid nasal sprays | Fluticasone (Flonase Allergy Relief), mometasone (Nasonex 24HR Allergy), triamcinolone (Nasacort Allergy 24HR) | Reduces swelling and controls hay fever symptoms |
| Decongestants | Tablets and nasal sprays | Shrinks swollen tissue in the nose |
| Leukotriene modifier | Montelukast (Singulair), by prescription | Blocks certain immune system chemicals, for people who can't use steroid sprays or antihistamines |

*Source: [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/pet-allergy/diagnosis-treatment/drc-20352198). Oral decongestants can raise blood pressure, and Mayo Clinic says using a decongestant spray more than three days in a row can make congestion worse.*

For chest symptoms, the [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/allergies/allergic-conditions/pet-allergies/) lists inhaled corticosteroids or bronchodilators, and for itchy eyes, antihistamine eye drops. The AAFA says your doctor may suggest taking medicine before you'll be around animals. Medicine has limits, though: the [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/conditions-treatments/allergies/pet-allergy) says it may relieve symptoms but isn't ideal as a long-term treatment.

## Allergy shots for cat and dog allergies

Allergy shots, also called immunotherapy, are the treatment that changes how your body reacts to cat or dog allergen. [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/pet-allergy/diagnosis-treatment/drc-20352198) describes the usual pet allergy schedule this way:

- **Build-up:** one or two shots a week, each with a very small dose of the animal protein, increasing usually over four to six months.
- **Maintenance:** a shot every four weeks for three to five years.

You don't need a separate course for each animal. [Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/allergy-shot-reactions/) says your provider tailors the cocktail of allergens in your shots to the allergies you have, so cat, dog, pollen and dust mites can be treated together. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/25194-allergy-shots) says anyone over 5 can get allergy shots.

Our [full guide to allergy shots](/learn-about-ilit/allergy-shots-and-allergy-immunotherapy) explains how they work and how safe they are, [How Often Do You Get Allergy Shots?](/blog/how-often-do-you-get-allergy-shots) counts the visits, and [What's in Allergy Shots?](/blog/whats-in-allergy-shots) covers ingredients and side effects.

## Do cat allergy shots work?

![A hand scratching under the chin of a white, orange and tabby cat with its eyes closed](https://res.cloudinary.com/qruprn0t/image/upload/v1795460745/pet-cat-dog-allergy-treatment-scratching-cat-chin.jpg)

For many people, yes, though the research behind them is thinner than you might expect. The [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/allergies/allergic-conditions/pet-allergies/) calls allergy shots an effective treatment that builds tolerance over time, and the AAFA says they can be very effective when other treatments and steps aren't working.

A [2018 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29182437/) of immunotherapy for pet allergies found it can reduce symptoms but pointed to the small number of high quality randomized trials. A [2020 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31132032/) from Spain followed 66 people, ages 9 to 59, through a year of high dose shots for cat or dog allergy. The authors concluded the shots had substantial clinical value in many of them. Rhinitis and asthma symptoms, quality of life and medicine use improved at six months and stayed better at 12, and people treated for cat allergy improved significantly more than those treated for dog allergy. That study used a fast build-up with an infusion pump, and about 8% to 9% of doses caused a whole body reaction.

[Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/25194-allergy-shots) says most people start noticing fewer symptoms six to eight months in, and the full benefit may take 12 months.

## Are there allergy tablets or drops for pet allergy?

Not as an approved option. Allergy tablets that go under the tongue are a form of sublingual immunotherapy (SLIT), and the four FDA approved tablets [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/25194-allergy-shots) lists treat dust mites (Odactra), mixed grasses (Oralair), Timothy grass (Grastek) and short ragweed (Ragwitek). None is for cats or dogs, which matches the [FDA's own list](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/allergenics/allergen-extract-sublingual-tablets). So for a cat or dog allergy, shots are the standard immunotherapy.

## ILIT allergy treatment for cat and dog allergy

ILIT allergy treatment puts FDA approved allergen extract into a lymph node in the groin (just below the waist, near the hip), guided by ultrasound. Using a lymph node is an off label route, which our [ILIT FAQ](/learn-about-ilit#faq-fda) explains. A course is typically three injections over about two months, which makes it a fast allergy treatment course next to years of shots, though not instant relief. In a [2008 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19001265/), people with grass pollen allergy who got three lymph node injections had about the same tolerance three years later as people who got 54 allergy shots, with fewer side effects.

For cats and dogs, the research is small and mixed:

- A [2012 cat allergy trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22464647/) found a 74 fold gain in nasal tolerance after three injections, with no side effects. But it used an engineered cat protein, not the standard extract U.S. practices use, and the study was funded in part by ImVisioN Therapeutics, which employed two of its authors.
- A [2021 placebo controlled trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8178859/) in South Korea gave ILIT allergy shots for dust mite, cat or dog allergy. People used less medicine at four months, but their symptom and quality of life scores were no better than placebo at four months or a year, and shortness of breath and wheezing were the most common whole body side effects, at 5.3%.
- A [2025 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12714667/) of seven earlier reviews found the clearest benefit for grass pollen and mixed allergies, with mostly mild reactions at the injection site and substantially fewer whole body reactions than standard shots. It said low quality evidence limits its conclusions.

If a practice offers ILIT for a cat or dog allergy, ask how many pet allergic patients it has treated and how they were doing a year later.

## Longer term options for pet allergy

Allergy medicine eases symptoms without changing the allergy. Allergy shots change it over three to five years, and the AAAAI calls them a proven long-term treatment. Allergy tablets don't cover pets. ILIT is the shortest immunotherapy course, with less research behind it for pet allergy so far.
