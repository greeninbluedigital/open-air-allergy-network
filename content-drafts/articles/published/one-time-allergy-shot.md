KEY TAKEAWAYS
- The shot people call a one-time or seasonal allergy shot is usually a steroid injection such as Kenalog, and its relief lasts weeks, not years.
- U.S. allergy guidelines recommend against depot steroid shots for hay fever because of their potential risks.
- Allergy shots change how your immune system reacts, but the course usually runs three to five years or longer.
- Some people stay symptom free after finishing allergy shots, while others need ongoing shots.
- ILIT isn't a single shot either. It's typically three injections over about two months.

FAQS
Q: What is the one-time allergy shot called?
A: Usually Kenalog, a brand name for triamcinolone acetonide, a long acting corticosteroid injection sold as Kenalog-40 and Kenalog-80. Its [FDA label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cf970688-cd77-b95c-4bd9-c541590e9722) says one dose may keep working for several weeks. It's a steroid, not immunotherapy, so the allergy itself is unchanged when it wears off.

Q: Is there a once a year allergy shot for humans?
A: Not one that lasts a full year. The steroid shot's [FDA label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cf970688-cd77-b95c-4bd9-c541590e9722) describes relief over several weeks, or through one pollen season for some hay fever patients whom other treatment hasn't helped. Allergy shots that retrain the immune system are given about once a month once you reach the maintenance phase, according to [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876).

Q: Do allergy shots have steroids?
A: No. An allergy shot holds a tiny amount of the allergens you react to, and the dose rises over time so your immune system learns not to react ([Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876)). A steroid shot such as Kenalog is a different drug that calms inflammation for a few weeks and leaves the allergy as it was.

Q: How long does it take for allergy shots to work?
A: Usually months. [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876) says symptoms usually improve during the first year, the most noticeable change often comes in the second, and by the third year most people no longer have bad reactions to their allergens. A shortened build-up schedule can bring relief sooner but raises the risk of a serious reaction.

Q: Can you have an allergic reaction to a steroid shot?
A: Yes, though it's rare. The [Kenalog label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cf970688-cd77-b95c-4bd9-c541590e9722) calls anaphylaxis rare with corticosteroid treatment, but serious cases, including deaths, have been reported after triamcinolone acetonide injections. Swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat, or trouble breathing after any shot, is a reason to call 911.

Q: Do allergy shots last a lifetime?
A: For some people. [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876) says that after a few years of successful treatment, some people have no allergy problems even after the shots stop, while others need ongoing shots to keep symptoms under control.

Q: Is ILIT allergy treatment a one-time shot?
A: No. In a [2008 trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2582048/) that compared it with allergy shots, ILIT patients got three injections over two months, against 54 allergy shots over three years in the other group. If you'd like to ask about it, use [Find a Provider](/find-an-ilit-provider) on OpenAirAllergyNetwork.com to locate an allergist who offers it.

If you've heard about a one-time allergy shot that gets people through a whole allergy season, you're most likely thinking of a steroid shot such as Kenalog, which can calm hay fever for several weeks before it wears off. No single injection changes the allergy itself. Immunotherapy does that, and it takes a series of doses: allergy shots usually run three to five years or longer, while ILIT (intralymphatic immunotherapy) is typically three injections over about two months.

Which one fits depends on whether you want a few quiet weeks this season or fewer bad seasons ahead. Below you'll find what the steroid shot does and why allergy specialists advise against it for hay fever, how long allergy shots take to work and how long they last, and how ILIT compares as a faster alternative to allergy shots, with allergy drops and tablets as another option.

## One-time allergy shot options at a glance

| Option | How many shots | How long relief lasts | What it changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steroid shot (Kenalog) | One | Several weeks, sometimes one pollen season | Calms symptoms. The allergy is unchanged when it wears off |
| Allergy shots | One to three a week for months, then about monthly, usually for three to five years or longer | Some people stay clear after stopping, others need ongoing shots | Teaches the immune system not to react |
| ILIT (intralymphatic immunotherapy) | Typically three, over about two months | In the main trial, similar to allergy shots three years after starting | Teaches the immune system not to react, in a shorter course |

*Sources: the Kenalog FDA label, Mayo Clinic, and a 2008 trial comparing ILIT with allergy shots (Senti et al., PNAS), each linked below.*

## The "one-time" allergy shot is usually a steroid shot

The shot that gets called a one-time, seasonal or yearly allergy shot is usually Kenalog, a brand of triamcinolone acetonide sold as Kenalog-40 and Kenalog-80. It's a corticosteroid, a drug that calms inflammation, and it doesn't contain any allergen. Its [FDA label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=cf970688-cd77-b95c-4bd9-c541590e9722) says the effect may last several weeks, and that some hay fever patients who haven't responded to pollen immunotherapy and other conventional treatment may get relief that lasts through the pollen season after one injection.

The same label limits that use to severe or incapacitating allergies that haven't improved with adequate trials of conventional treatment. The joint [AAAAI and ACAAI rhinitis guideline](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaci.2020.07.007) goes further, saying depot steroid injections aren't recommended for hay fever because of their potential risks, and it names steroid nasal sprays as the preferred single treatment for ongoing nasal allergies. Our [Kenalog guide](/blog/kenalog-shot-for-allergies) covers the side effects and why repeat shots add up.

## Allergy shots: how long they take, and how long they last

Allergy shots are immunotherapy. Each one holds a tiny amount of what you're allergic to, and the dose rises over time so your immune system learns not to react, as [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/tests-procedures/allergy-shots/about/pac-20392876) explains. The build-up phase generally takes three to six months, with shots typically one to three times a week. Maintenance shots follow about once a month, generally for three to five years or longer, and you stay in the clinic for 30 minutes after each one in case of a reaction.

Mayo Clinic says relief builds over years: symptoms usually improve during the first year, the most noticeable change often comes in the second, and by the third year most people no longer have bad reactions to their allergens. In some cases the build-up is shortened by giving several doses per visit, which gets you to maintenance and relief sooner but raises the risk of a serious reaction. Our guide to [how often you get allergy shots](/blog/how-often-do-you-get-allergy-shots) counts the visits on each schedule.

## Can allergies be cured permanently?

Not reliably, and not with one shot. Immunotherapy comes closest, because it works on the immune response rather than the symptoms. A [2025 review of the ILIT research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12714667/) describes allergen immunotherapy as the only therapy currently able to change the natural course of allergic disease.

How long that change lasts varies by person. Mayo Clinic says that after a few years of successful allergy shots, some people have no allergy problems even after treatment stops, while others need ongoing shots to keep symptoms under control.

## ILIT allergy treatment: the shortest course, not a single shot

ILIT puts the allergen directly into a lymph node, usually in the groin (just below the waist, near the hip), with ultrasound used to guide the needle. In a [2008 trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2582048/) that compared it with allergy shots, 165 people with grass pollen allergy got either 54 allergy shots over three years or three ILIT injections over two months. Within four months, the ILIT group already tolerated more grass pollen in a nasal test. Three years after starting, that tolerance was about the same as the allergy shot group's, and ILIT caused fewer side effects. The paper discloses that its corresponding author is named as the inventor on an ILIT patent.

The wider research is more cautious. The 2025 review pulled together seven systematic reviews and found the benefit clearest for grass pollen and mixed allergies, with less effect for dust mite allergy alone. It rated six of the seven reviews low in methodological quality and one very low, and found long term results limited. Side effects were mostly mild reactions where the needle goes in, with substantially fewer whole body reactions than allergy shots. ILIT uses FDA approved allergen extracts given by an off label route, which our [ILIT FAQ](/learn-about-ilit#faq-fda) explains.

## Longer term options

**Allergy medicine** handles day to day symptoms. The [ACAAI](https://acaai.org/allergies/allergic-conditions/hay-fever/) calls steroid nasal sprays the single most effective drug class for hay fever, and they're designed to avoid the side effects of steroids taken by mouth or by injection.

**Allergy shots** change the allergy, usually over three to five years or longer of regular visits.

**Allergy tablets** dissolve under the tongue, and the first doses are given by an allergist under medical observation. The [Asthma and Allergy Foundation of America](https://aafa.org/allergies/allergy-treatments/) lists tablets for dust mites (Odactra), grass (Grastek and Oralair) and ragweed (Ragwitek).

**ILIT** is typically three injections over about two months, which makes it a fast allergy treatment course next to years of shots, though it isn't instant relief. The research is promising but smaller than for allergy shots, and results vary from person to person.

Our [comparison of allergy shots, allergy drops and ILIT](/learn-about-ilit/allergy-shots-vs-allergy-drops-vs-ilit) lays out all three side by side.
